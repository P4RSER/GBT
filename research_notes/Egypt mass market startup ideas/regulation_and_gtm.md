## 1. Company setup: legal forms, GAFI process, Startup Charter, ITIDA, offshore holding companies

### Takeaway
A local LLC or single-person company (OPC) can be incorporated through GAFI in about 7 business days, or about 2 with a EGP 10,000 premium service. The national Startup Charter launched in Feb 2026. It brings:
- an official definition of a startup;
- a classification certificate;
- a simplified turnover tax of 0.4–1.5% for revenue under EGP 20m;
- specialized tax offices, 90-day liquidation and easier access to tenders.

Most of these incentives are procedural, and implementation is early: 61 startups were accredited by May 2026. Offshore holding companies (Delaware, BVI, ADGM) are driven by investors. They matter mainly when a startup raises foreign VC.

**Offshore holding companies**
- **Why startups go offshore:**
  - The decision is mainly led by investors. It is driven by regulatory and legislative factors and by payment facilitation.
  - **Delaware and BVI** are the most common holding jurisdictions. Operations stay in Egypt because costs are lower.
  - Egypt lacks frameworks for many alternative investment instruments.
  - Investors can avoid Egyptian capital-gains tax on exit if the holding company is, for example, in Delaware.
  
  — [Enterprise, "Why some Egyptian startups prefer to set up shop abroad"](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/whatsnext/why-some-egyptian-startups-prefer-to-set-up-shop-abroad/) (date not visible)
- A "Delaware flip" (moving an **ADGM SPV** holding into a new Delaware corporation) is emerging in MENA when US lead investors come in. — [Taylor Wessing, VC MENA 2025](https://vcmena2025.taylorwessing.com/introduction/delaware-flips)
- Swvl, Widebot and Taager keep Egyptian engineering teams but have holding companies abroad. The Charter is framed as a response to founders moving to Riyadh and Dubai. — [Launchbase Africa, 9 Feb 2026](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/02/09/can-egypts-new-startup-charter-stop-founders-fleeing-to-riyadh-and-dubai/); policy discussion of "reverse establishment" in [MFW4A](https://www.mfw4a.org/publication/entrepreneuship-egypt-case-study-policy-recommendation-reverse-establishment-startups)

### Inferences
- **Choosing a structure:** a bootstrapped or angel-funded team selling to Egyptian consumers and SMBs should start with a local LLC or OPC. It is fast and cheap, and merchant onboarding with payment gateways requires a registered entity. An offshore holding company becomes relevant only when foreign VCs require it. It can be added later through a flip, at a legal cost.
- **What the Charter is worth now:** the most useful immediate benefit for a small team is probably the simplified 0.4–1.5% turnover tax with dedicated tax offices. That tax looks like Egypt's general simplified regime for businesses under EGP 20m, routed through certification, rather than a startup tax holiday.
- **Implementation is early:** 61 accreditations in about 3 months is small against a 5,000-startup target. Founders should not count on Charter-specific benefits (grants, procurement access, customs) materialising quickly.

## 2. Data protection: Personal Data Protection Law (Law 151 of 2020) and its Executive Regulations

### Takeaway
The PDPL became operational when its Executive Regulations were issued (Decree No. 816 of 2025, dated 1 Nov 2025). The **one-year grace period ends 31 Oct 2026, four weeks from today**. After that:
- controllers and processors need PDPC licences or permits, with fees tiered by number of records (small datasets are fee-exempt);
- any cross-border transfer, which includes hosting data abroad, needs its own licence;
- breaches must be notified within 72 hours;
- sensitive-data or transfer violations carry fines up to EGP 5m plus prison.

This is the most universal compliance item for any consumer or SMB app.

### Cited Findings
- **The decree:** the Executive Regulations were issued by **Decree No. 816 of 2025 on 1 November 2025**. — [Baker McKenzie, Jan 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/egypt-important-data-protection-update); [Shalakany, Dec 2025](https://shalakany.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/New-Executive-Regulations-for-Egypts-Personal-Data-Protection-Law-.pdf)
  - Arabic sources call it a **ministerial decree by the Minister of Communications and IT**, published in the Official Gazette and in force the day after publication. — [RecordingLaw (Arabic)](https://www.recordinglaw.com/ar/world-laws/world-data-privacy-laws/egypt-data-privacy-laws/)
  - One English summary calls it a "Prime Ministerial Decree". This conflicts with the above; the ministerial version matches the law's assignment of the regulations to the competent minister.
- **Five-year delay:** the 2020 law was largely inoperative for five years because the regulations had not been issued. — [CADE](https://cadeproject.org/updates/title-egypt-activates-data-protection-law-with-implementing-regulations-after-five-year-delay/)
- **What the regulations add:** enforceable operating standards, graduated licensing regimes, and detailed procedural and documentation duties. Collection, processing, retention and protection of personal data are governed by **licences and permits issued by the Personal Data Protection Center (PDPC)**. — [Access Partnership](https://accesspartnership.com/opinion/egypt-finalises-executive-regulations-to-the-personal-data-protection-law-pdpl/); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/overview-of-the-executive-regulations-of-the-egyptian-personal-data-protection-law/)
- **Deadline:** controllers and processors have a **one-year grace period ending 31 October 2026**. Enforcement is expected from **1 November 2026**. — [Kennedys, 2026](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/); [ADSERO](https://adsero.me/executive-regulations-issued-for-egypts-personal-data-protection-law-business-impact-and-compliance-considerations/); [Chambers article](https://chambers.com/articles/a-first-look-at-egypt-s-personal-data-protection-executive-regulations)
- **Breach notification:** notify the PDPC **within 72 hours**, and tell affected individuals **within 3 working days** of notifying the PDPC. — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/egypt-important-data-protection-update); [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/)
- **Data Protection Officer:** some organisations must appoint a **PDPC-registered DPO**, depending on the scale and sensitivity of their processing. — same sources
- **Licence fees by number of records** (as summarised; the bands look garbled in places and I could not check them against the regulation text):

  | Records held | Fee |
  |---|---|
  | 1–100,000 | **Exempt** |
  | ~100k–200k | EGP 200 |
  | ~200k–300k | EGP 300 |
  | ~300k–1m | EGP 400–1,000 |
  | 1m–2m | EGP 5,000–50,000 |
  | 2m–5m | EGP 60,000–500,000 |
  | Over 5m | EGP 666,666 per year (statutory cap EGP 2m over a 3-year term) |

  — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/compliance/1845112/from-compliance-to-capital-how-egypts-pdpl-shapes-data-valuation); [DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/egypt-pdpc-issues-executive-regulations-personal-data); [Chambers article](https://chambers.com/articles/a-first-look-at-egypt-s-personal-data-protection-executive-regulations)
- **Fee reductions:** a controller-only or processor-only licence gets a **50% fee reduction**. A **cross-border-transfer licence costs 50%** of the controller or processor licence fee. — same sources
- **Cross-border transfers:** controllers must get a **separate PDPC licence before transferring personal data outside Egypt**. The application must state the destination, purpose, data types and security measures. — [Consortio Law Firm](https://consortiolawfirm.com/cross-border-data-transfer-egypt-pdpl/)
- **Penalties:**
  - violations involving **sensitive data** or **unlawful cross-border transfers**: **imprisonment and fines of EGP 500,000–5,000,000**;
  - general violations such as unauthorised collection or processing: **EGP 100,000–1,000,000**.
  
  — [Signzy PDPL glossary](https://www.signzy.com/regulation-glossary/personal-data-protection-law-151); [LawLab Africa](https://research.lawlab.africa/egypt/)

### Inferences
- **COMPLICATION, applies to almost every app from 1 Nov 2026:** a startup that hosts on AWS (Bahrain or UAE), Firebase, Supabase or another foreign cloud is transferring data across borders, so it needs a PDPC cross-border licence on top of its controller licence. Budget for licence paperwork, an Arabic privacy notice and consent flows, a data inventory, a breach-response procedure and possibly an outsourced DPO.
- **Fee exemption is not a licence exemption:** the exemption for 100,000 records or fewer appears to cover only the fee. A small MVP probably still needs to hold a licence or permit, but at near-zero cost. Verify this.
- **Highest-risk categories:** apps handling financial data (personal-finance, SMS parsing), health data (telemedicine, pharmacy) or children's data (edtech, tutoring) carry the top penalty tier, if these are "sensitive" under the law (see Gaps). They need explicit consent and the heaviest controls.
- **Enforcement uncertainty:** whether the PDPC can enforce at scale right after 31 Oct 2026 is unknown. Being licensed may still become a requirement in B2B sales and in bank or telco partnerships.

### Gaps
- **Sensitive data definition: UNVERIFIED in this session.** The law is widely reported (for example, in the DLA Piper guide) to define sensitive data as including health, genetic, biometric, **financial** and religious data, political opinions and security status, and to treat **all children's data as sensitive**. Verify at the [DLA Piper Egypt guide](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=EG).
- **CBE carve-out: UNVERIFIED.** The law is understood to exclude data held by the CBE and entities under its supervision, except money-transfer and exchange companies. If so, this matters for bank partners but not for unlicensed startups.
- **Not found:**
  - whether there is any blanket data-localisation duty, beyond the transfer licence;
  - sector rules, such as CBE cloud and outsourcing rules for financial institutions;
  - whether the PDPC licensing portal is live and how long licences take to issue;
  - specific rules on children's consent age and parental consent;
  - how "permits" differ from "licences" in practice.

## 3. Fintech and payments regulation: CBE, FRA and InstaPay/IPN

### Takeaway
- **CBE licensing:** since **17 June 2025**, every payment activity needs a CBE PSO/PSP licence with tiered capital. This includes "**access to payment account information**", which covers bank-account aggregation.
- **No open banking yet:** formal open-banking APIs and third-party access rules are still under development.
- **FRA licensing:** non-bank lending, BNPL and consumer finance need an FRA licence. The minimum capital for fintech startups has been cut to **EGP 15m**, and new microfinance and consumer-finance licences are reportedly paused.
- **What a small team should do:** integrate licensed gateways, which already offer InstaPay, wallets, Meeza, cards, payment links and QR, rather than handle funds or account data directly. I found no public InstaPay/IPN API for unlicensed third parties.

### Inferences
- **BLOCKER: account-aggregating personal-finance app.** Pulling bank or wallet balances and transactions through APIs falls under "access to payment account information", which needs a CBE PSP licence with minimum capital. No mandated open-banking APIs exist yet. This is not feasible for a small team in 2026 except as a vendor to a licensed bank or PSP.
- **COMPLICATION, not clearly a CBE licence matter: SMS-reading personal-finance app.** I found no rule that classes on-device parsing of a user's own bank SMS as a licensed payment activity. However:
  - the data is very likely "sensitive" financial data under the PDPL, so it needs a licence and explicit consent;
  - it depends on Google Play granting the SMS permission;
  - it cannot work on iOS (see Section 5).
- **BLOCKER: lending, BNPL, consumer finance or microfinance.** These need an FRA licence (at least EGP 15m capital) and new licences may be paused. Workable models are referral or distribution partnerships with licensed lenders, or SaaS sold to them. Avoid looking like an unlicensed intermediary that arranges credit.
- **Wallets and stored value:** holding customer funds, issuing wallets or running payouts would need CBE licensing, either directly or through a bank partner. A small team should design products where the licensed gateway or bank holds the money.
- **Accepting payments is easy through licensed gateways:** the startup needs a registered company and a bank account for merchant KYC. InstaPay is reachable through gateways, not directly.

### Gaps
- **Not found: CBE details.** Exact minimum capital and guarantees for PSP Categories A and B; whether payment initiation is a separate activity; whether payment-facilitator or sub-merchant models are allowed; whether the CBE sandbox is open to new applicants in 2026.
- **Not found: InstaPay for businesses.** Fees and limits for merchant collections through IPN; whether a direct IPN or InstaPay API exists for licensed PSPs only; whether individuals may use personal InstaPay addresses or QR for business.
- **Not found: FRA rules.** Insurtech licensing and FRA microfinance rules in detail; whether the reported licence pause exempts digital-only fintech; any explicit regulator statement on SMS-parsing personal-finance apps.
- **Not found: e-wallets.** The e-wallet model (bank-led wallets partnered with telcos) was not confirmed in this session.
- **UNVERIFIED:** the CBE Law 194/2020 is understood to ban issuing, trading or promoting crypto without a CBE licence.

## 4. Sector rules: health, consumer protection, e-signature, ride-hailing, tutoring, real estate, ID/passport photos

### Takeaway
- **Online medicine sales:** a legal grey zone. The regulator and the pharmacists' syndicate are hostile, and a dedicated e-pharmacy law is being prepared.
- **Telemedicine:** permitted but loosely regulated.
- **Private tutoring centres:** officially ordered closed, but widespread. Licensing proposals are still unresolved.
- **Real-estate brokers:** must now register and be licensed through a GOEIC registry. The deadline to regularise was July 2026.
- **E-signatures:** legally binding under Law 15/2004.
- **Not researched (search budget ran out):** e-commerce duties under the Consumer Protection Law, the ride-hailing law and official ID/passport photo specifications.

### Cited Findings
**Online pharmacy and e-prescriptions**
- **No dedicated framework:** Egypt has **no specific legal framework for e-pharmacies**. The general ban on dispensing prescription medicine without a valid prescription applies online too. Buying medicine online is "not illegal per se". — [Lexis Middle East, 19 Dec 2025](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/eJournal/2025-12-19_17/en)
- **Conflicting claim:** a vendor blog says "Ministerial Decree 238/2023" sets e-pharmacy licensing requirements. This is unverified and contradicts the Lexis analysis. — [MedSoftwares blog](https://www.medsoftwares.com/news/pharmacy-software-egypt-healthcare-2026)
- **Prescriptions are still on paper:** prescribing remains largely paper-based, and no national e-prescribing platform has been deployed. — [Lexis Middle East, 19 Dec 2025](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/eJournal/2025-12-19_17/en); [Expat Focus](https://www.expatfocus.com/egypt/guide/egypt-prescriptions-and-medications)
- **EDA activity:**
  - The EDA carried out **7,200+ licensing procedures** for pharmacies, drug stores and storage outlets in 2025. — [EDA](https://edaegypt.gov.eg/en/media-center/news/egyptian-drug-authority-achieves-significant-milestones-in-licensing-pharmaceutical-establishments-during-2025/)
  - The EDA has issued online promotional and educational guidelines (content not reviewed). — [EDA guidelines PDF](https://www.edaegypt.gov.eg/media/thblbqzk/online-guidelines-final-2.pdf)
- **Regulatory stance:**
  - The EDA says it is tackling the "chaos" of medicine-selling apps. It has assigned pharmacists to monitor them and is preparing a **dedicated e-pharmacy law** with the Ministry of Justice (article date not verified). — [Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/5731349)
  - The Pharmacists' Syndicate filed complaints against a medicine-selling app — [El Watan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/5062088) — and against Amazon in Jan 2022 — [Al Jazeera](https://www.ajnet.me/health/2022/1/13/%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%86%d9%82%d8%a7%d8%a8%d8%a9-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%b9%d8%a7%d9%85%d8%a9-%d9%84%d9%84%d8%b5%d9%8a%d8%a7%d8%af%d9%84%d8%a9-%d8%aa%d8%aa%d9%82%d8%af%d9%85-%d8%a8%d8%b4%d9%83%d9%88%d9%89).
  - The Senate Health Committee has expressed reservations about marketing medicine through apps — [Al Mal](https://almalnews.com/1207883/«صحة-الشيوخ»-تبدي-تحفظها-على-تسويق-الدواء/) — and the head of the medicines trade division warns against buying through apps — [Al Mal](https://almalnews.com/1619146/رئيس-شعبة-الدواء-يحذر-من-شراء-الأدوية/).

**Telemedicine**
- **Legal basis:** telemedicine is legally recognised and endorsed by the Ministry of Health and Population. The laws that apply include:
  - the E-Signature Law **15/2004**, which makes e-signatures legally binding like handwritten ones;
  - the Medical Syndicate Law 45/1969, whose ethics duties extend to telemedicine;
  - the **Cybercrime Law 175/2018**, which penalises unauthorised access and data breaches;
  - the PDPL 151/2020.
  
  — [Andersen Egypt, Telemedicine in Egypt](https://eg.andersen.com/telemedicine-in-egypt/)
- **Open issues:** fair tariffs for remote consultations, cross-border licensing, GAHAR 2025 accreditation standards and the proposed malpractice law. — [Mondaq, Telemedicine in Egypt](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/healthcare/1428986/telemedicine-in-egypt-compliance-challenges-and-growth)
- **National platform:** on **14 Sep 2025** the Egypt Healthcare Authority unveiled a national telemedicine platform. It connects Egyptian doctors abroad with patients under Universal Health Insurance. — [ME Observer](https://meobserver.news/?p=131729) (attribution best-effort)

**Private tutoring**
- **Current position:** the Ministry of Education ordered private tutoring centres closed indefinitely, but they keep operating covertly.
  - Legislation is being discussed between the Teachers' Syndicate and the Ministry on a **professional practice licence** for teachers. Centres employing unlicensed teachers would be liable.
  - Centres today are unlicensed, untaxed and not inspected.
  
  — [Al Bawaba News](https://www.albawabhnews.com/5387608); [Parlmany](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/242696/اقتصاد-خارج-الرقابة) (article dates not verified)
- **Enforcement and demand:** local closures continue, for example **21 tutoring centres closed in Ismailia**. — [Al Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5525811). Parents have called for centres to be licensed. — [Parlmany](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/246318/%D8%A3%D9%88%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A3%D9%85%D9%88%D8%B1-%D9%8A%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A8%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%AE%D9%8A%D8%B5-%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%83%D8%B2-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B5%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%8A%D8%A9)
- **History:**
  - In **Nov 2022** the government dropped plans to license tutoring centres, choosing to crack down on "bad practices" and promote its own supplementary classes. — [The National, 3 Nov 2022](https://thenational-the-national-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/mena/egypt/2022/11/03/egypt-backtracks-on-licensing-private-tutorial-centres)
  - In **Jan 2023** the minister said teachers may need licences. — [Enterprise, 24 Jan 2023](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2023/01/24/teachers-could-need-to-get-licensed-soon-and-so-could-social-media-firms/)
  - The private tutoring market was valued at **EGP 47bn in FY2021/22**. — [Enterprise](https://enterprise.press/?p=215744) (attribution best-effort)
- **School licensing:** in **Mar 2025** the Ministry ordered an unlicensed international school in New Cairo to close at the end of the year. This shows active enforcement of education licensing. — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Business/Egyptian-Government-to-Shut-Down-New-Cairo-British-International-School-for-License-Violations)

**Real-estate brokerage**
- **Registry:** **Ministerial Decision No. 578 of 2025** (Ministry of Investment and External Trade) defines the types and categories of real-estate brokers to be entered in an official registry. **GOEIC** is the registering authority. — [Invest-Gate](https://invest-gate.me/features/real-estate-brokers-registry-toward-a-more-transparent-and-investment-driven-market/)
- **Licensing:** reports say "Law No. 21 of 2022" requires brokers to hold a government licence, with a **July 2026 deadline** to regularise. The law number is unverified. — [Property Finder blog](https://www.propertyfinder.eg/blog/?p=27106); [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=44637)
- **Training:** accredited training is a licence prerequisite. It covers the legal framework, code of conduct, contract drafting and AML/CFT, and Property Finder delivers it with GOEIC. — [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=44637)
- **Proposed amendments:** brokerages would register electronically, with rules on the fees they charge and how fees are collected. — [Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/?p=543839)

**ID and passport photos**
- **Egyptian passport photo rules** (from a commercial photo-app site, not an official source): head straight and not tilted, looking at the camera; neutral expression, with a slight closed-mouth smile allowed; clear, non-reflective prescription glasses only; religious head covering allowed if the whole face is visible. — [Smartphone-ID](https://www.smartphone-id.com/ar-ae/متطلبات-الصورة-جواز-السفر-المصرية)
- **National ID application:** a form bought at the civil-status office, an electronic birth certificate (first-time applicants) and a recent utility bill. Applicants must be Egyptian and at least 15. — [Al Dostor, 2025](https://www.dostor.org/5257009). No official photo specification was returned. The Civil Status Organization site (cso.moi.gov.eg) is the place to verify.

### Inferences
- **HIGH RISK: online pharmacy and medicine delivery.** Only viable as software for licensed pharmacies, such as ordering or inventory tools, or OTC-only delivery from licensed outlets. Prescription medicines need a valid prescription, and a hostile regulator is drafting a specific law.
- **COMPLICATION: telemedicine.** Feasible with syndicate-registered doctors. Health data is sensitive under the PDPL, and pricing and liability are unsettled.
- **RISK: SaaS for physical tutoring centres** (bookings, payments, attendance). The customers are officially unlicensed or ordered closed, which exposes the product to enforcement swings. Online self-study or teacher tools carry less exposure, but online tutoring rules were not found.
- **COMPLICATION: property brokerage or commission platforms.** A platform that takes broker-like commissions may need registration or licensing as a broker under the new GOEIC regime. A pure listings or SaaS model is safer.
- **UNKNOWN: AI or online ID/passport photo apps.** Demand depends on whether civil-status and passport offices accept user-supplied photos or take them on site. This was not established.

### Gaps
- **Consumer Protection Law 181/2018 e-commerce duties: not researched. UNVERIFIED background:** the law and its 2019 regulations are understood to require distance sellers to disclose their identity, product details and full price including taxes, give Arabic-language information or invoices, and allow returns within about 14 days. Verify with the Consumer Protection Agency (cpa.gov.eg).
- **Ride-hailing Law 87/2018: not researched. UNVERIFIED background:** the law is understood to require ride-hailing platforms to be licensed, to pay licence fees, and to keep user and trip data available to authorities on request. Verify the current rules.
- **E-signature:** only the basic legal-validity point is sourced. Who can issue e-signature certificates (ITIDA-licensed certification providers) and whether remote e-signing is allowed for consumer contracts were not verified.
- **Medical liability law:** the status of the 2025 medical liability / patient safety law was not verified.
- **Online tutoring:** 2025–2026 rules or crackdowns on online tutoring platforms, and the dates of the closure orders above, were not found.
- **Photos:** official specifications for national-ID and passport photos (size, background, biometric capture at the office) and whether online or AI-generated photos are accepted were not found.
