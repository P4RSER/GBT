## 1. Demographics: population, age, households, urban/rural, regions, literacy, language

### Takeaway
CAPMAS counted 109 million residents on 9 May 2026 (108.6M at the start of 2026, 94.8M at the 2017 census). The net increase is about 1.3–1.4M people a year. About 70.5M people are aged 15+ (CBE, end-2025), which implies roughly 35% are under 15. UN-based datasets such as DataReportal assume a population of about 118.8M, roughly 10M more than CAPMAS, so mixing the two inflates "user" counts. Household counts, the urban/rural split, governorate populations, literacy and app-language data could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Egypt's domestic population reached 109 million on Saturday 9 May 2026 (CAPMAS statement). The population clock is linked to the Ministry of Health births/deaths registry and recorded a net +1 million in 267 days, between Aug 2025 and May 2026 — CAPMAS, as surfaced in search results citing [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/572647.aspx) and [Cairo Governorate](https://cairo.gov.eg/en/news/news-eng/2025/1aa7f2e31cf84a1f931d520fa095dd36) (which of the two pages carried it is unverified)
- The population clock showed 108,750,000 on 28 Feb 2026, up 750,000 in 196 days — [Egypt Today](https://www.egypttoday.com/Article/1/145282/CAPMAS-Egypt-records-population-increase-of-750K-in-196-days)
- The domestic population was 108.6M at the start of 2026, against 94.8M at the 2017 census (+13.8M in nine years). CAPMAS projects Egypt will be the world's 11th most populous country by 2050 — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/572647.aspx)
- Citizens aged 15+ numbered 70.5M at end-2025. This is the denominator of the CBE financial-inclusion index — CBE via [Hapi Journal](https://hapijournal.com/2026/02/17/البنك-المركزي-77-6-معدل-الشمول-المالي-في-م/)
- DataReportal, end-2025: 98.2M internet users (82.7% of population), 20.6M people offline (17.3%), and 121M cellular connections (102% of population) — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: Egypt](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-egypt)

### Inferences
- **Growth.** 1M people in 267 days is about 1.37M a year (~1.26%/yr). 750K in 196 days is about 1.40M a year. That is roughly 3,700–3,800 net new residents per day, so demand for first phones, first accounts, education and jobs keeps growing every year.
- **Age structure.** 70.5M adults ÷ ~108.6M residents means about 65% are aged 15+ and about 35% are under 15. This is derived; I did not retrieve a CAPMAS age table.
- **Base mismatch.** DataReportal's own ratios imply a population of about 118.7–118.9M (98.2/0.827; 121/1.02; 51.6/0.434). That is about 10M (~9%) above CAPMAS. Applying the 82.7% internet penetration to the CAPMAS base gives about 90M internet users, not 98.2M. For TAM work, use CAPMAS for resident counts and treat DataReportal's absolute counts as upper bounds.
- **Urban vs rural signal.** This is the only proxy found. In Aug 2026, urban inflation was 14.5% against 12.7% nationwide (§3), so rural inflation was lower; food-heavy rural baskets benefited from falling food prices.

  - the literacy rate
  - Arabic vs English usage in apps (no source of any kind found)

## 2. Income and spending: HIECS, poverty, middle class, category shares, remittances

### Takeaway
The latest CAPMAS household survey with headline averages found here is HIECS 2019/20: average household income EGP 69.1K/yr and spending EGP 61.9K/yr. HIECS 2021/22 microdata put mean household expenditure at about EGP 72–77K/yr. All of these predate the 2022–2024 devaluations and inflation surge, so they badly understate today's nominal EGP figures and overstate USD purchasing power. The World Bank puts national poverty at 33.5% in 2021/22, with poverty at the $4.20/day line up about 5pp between 2022 and 2024. Spending shares by category, middle-class size and remittance-receiving households were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- **HIECS 2019/20 (older, superseded).** Average annual household spending was EGP 61.9K, up from 53.7K in 2017/18. Average annual household income was EGP 69.1K, up from EGP 60.4K in 2017/18 — CAPMAS via [El Watan News](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/5092762)
- **HIECS 2021/22 microdata (CAPMAS + ERF).**
  - Mean total household expenditure EGP 76,721.87/yr (SD 40,940.79) — [ERF Data Portal](https://erfdataportal.com/index.php/catalog/309)
  - CAPMAS metadata for the "Survey of Income, Expenditure and Consumption 2021" shows mean annual family expenditure of EGP 72,395.8 (SD 40,080) — [CAPMAS microdata catalog](https://censusinfo.capmas.gov.eg/Metadata-en-v4.2/index.php/catalog/747/overview)
  - The two values differ. They are likely sample-level statistics on different variables, not official published headline averages.
- **World Bank poverty figures.**
  - National poverty rate 33.5% in 2021/22.
  - Poverty at the lower-middle-income international line (US$4.20/day, 2021 PPP) rose by about 5 percentage points between 2022 and 2024 — [World Bank document](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099558104212527926/pdf/IDU-353e3c35-372f-4190-b161-88f749c99df0.pdf); [World Bank Egypt overview](https://worldbank.org/en/country/egypt)
  - Poverty at $4.20/day was estimated at 7.1% in 2021 — reported in the same search results, possibly from an aggregator ([Statistics of the World](https://statisticsoftheworld.com/country/egypt/poverty-rate)); verify before use.
  - Do **not** use the search summarizer's own extrapolation of "38–39% national poverty"; it wrongly mixed the national and international poverty lines.
- **Minimum wage.** EGP 7,000/month (2025) and EGP 8,000/month from Jul 2026. See §3 for sources.

### Inferences
- **USD terms.** HIECS 2021/22 mean household spending of EGP 72–77K is about $3.8–4.9K/yr at that period's exchange rates ([UNVERIFIED]: ~15.7 EGP/$ before the Mar 2022 devaluation, ~18–19 after). At about 50.6 EGP/$ (mid-2026), the same nominal sum is about $1.4–1.5K. Nominal incomes have risen since then, but the 3x+ fall in the pound means USD purchasing power per household has almost certainly declined.
- **How stale HIECS is.** A single minimum-wage earner in 2026 (EGP 8,000 × 12 = EGP 96K/yr) now out-earns the average 2021/22 household's total spending, which shows how much nominal values have inflated. Do not size markets on HIECS nominal values without inflating them.
- **People below the poverty line.** At 33.5% (2021/22) of about 108.6M, at least ~36M people are below the national poverty line, and the World Bank trend points up after 2022. This segment is reachable through free, ad-supported, government or B2B2C-funded services, not paid consumer subscriptions.
- **Affordability heuristic (my judgment).** For a minimum-wage household (~$158/month), a mass-market consumer app probably needs to cost about 1% of monthly income or less, i.e. EGP 50–100/month, or be paid per use.

### Gaps
- No official CAPMAS release of the HIECS 2021/22 headline averages or poverty rate was found. [UNVERIFIED] pointer: the official CAPMAS poverty rate was 29.7% in 2019/20 (32.5% in 2017/18).
- Not retrieved:
  - spending shares by category (food, housing/utilities, health, education including private tutoring, transport, communications)
  - income quintiles
  - size of the middle and upper-middle class
  - Cairo/Alexandria vs Upper Egypt and urban vs rural income gaps
- Remittance-receiving households (share, by governorate) and remittance inflows were not retrieved. [UNVERIFIED] pointer: CBE reported record remittances of about $36.5B in FY2024/25.

## 3. Macro context 2023–2026: inflation, EGP/USD, interest rates, wages, consumer behavior

### Takeaway
Egypt entered 2026 disinflating and easing: core inflation was 11.2% in Jan 2026, and the CBE cut 100bps on 12 Feb 2026 to 19.0% deposit / 20.0% lending. Then the US–Israel strikes on Iran from 28 Feb 2026 set off a chain of shocks:
- more than $3B of portfolio outflows
- a 6–8% fall in the pound, from about 47.9–48.0 to 51–51.8 per dollar
- fuel price rises of 14–22% on 10 Mar 2026
- renewed inflation: urban 15.2% in March, 14.9% in July and 14.5% in August 2026; nationwide 12.7% in August

The CBE has held rates since, and the minimum wage rises to EGP 8,000 (~$158) from July 2026. Consumers face their second cost-of-living squeeze in about 30 months.

### Inferences
- **Minimum wage in USD (at ~50.6):** EGP 7,000 ≈ $138/month (2025 level); EGP 8,000 ≈ $158/month (from Jul 2026).
- **Real rates.** The real policy rate is about 19% deposit − 14.5% urban inflation ≈ +4.5pp (Aug 2026). Savers earn positive real returns, while consumer credit (installments/BNPL) is expensive to fund at a cost of funds of about 20%+. Startups should avoid models that require them to finance receivables unless a bank or BNPL partner carries the balance sheet.
- **Inflation target.** The CBE's pre-war target of 7% ± 2% by Q4 2026 looks out of reach given 14.5% urban inflation in Aug 2026, so rates are likely to stay high near term. This is my inference, not a sourced forecast.
- **FX volatility.** There have been two FX shocks in about 30 months: the Mar 2024 float ([UNVERIFIED]: about 30.9 → ~49–50 per dollar on 6 Mar 2024) and the Mar 2026 war shock (48 → ~51.5). Price in EGP with built-in periodic repricing, and treat USD-linked costs (cloud, AI APIs, app-store and ad payments) as a margin risk.
- **Consumer behavior.** The only evidence retrieved is indirect:
  - after the May 2026 price increases, the regulator ordered ultra-cheap telecom bundles, a sign of affordability stress (§4)
  - Fawry's prepaid cards rose 116% and Financial Services revenue 65% in 2026, showing demand for digital money and credit products (§5)

  - on 6 Mar 2024 the pound was floated and rates hiked 600bps to 27.25%/28.25%
  - the IMF EFF was enlarged to about $8B in Mar 2024

- Not retrieved: the real wage index, private-sector wage growth and unemployment.
- No direct evidence was found on trading down, brand switching or BNPL/installment usage, for example FRA consumer-finance statistics or valU/Sympl/Halan/Contact volumes.

## 4. Connectivity: internet, mobile, devices, prices, social media, apps

### Takeaway
At end-2025 Egypt had about 98M internet users (82.7% on the DataReportal base, or about 90M on the CAPMAS base) and 121M mobile connections, but only 51.6M social-media "user identities". Android dominates with 88–91% of mobile web traffic in 2026; iOS is a 9–12% affluent niche. Telecom prices rose 9–15% from 6 May 2026: fixed broadband now costs about EGP 1.7–2.0 per GB (EGP 287 for 150GB), and NTRA ordered EGP 5 mobile and EGP 150 fixed entry bundles. Platform-level social reach, time online and app rankings were not retrieved.

### Inferences
- **Fixed broadband cost.** About EGP 1.70–1.98 per GB. 150GB costs about $5.7/month and the EGP 150 entry bundle about $3.0 (at ~50.6). The 150GB tier is 3.6% of the EGP 8,000 minimum wage: cheap in USD, but meaningful for low-income households.
- **Multi-SIM.** Mobile connections (121M) are about 1.7x the adult population (70.5M), so multi-SIM use is common and unique subscribers are far fewer than connections.
- **Heavy vs light users.** Social media identities (51.6M) are only about 53% of internet users (98.2M). Many "internet users" are probably light or messaging-centric users. This is unverified because WhatsApp-specific reach data was not retrieved.
- **Build Android-first and lightweight.** About 90% of mobile web traffic is Android, and Android 11 or older is at least 14%. Keep app size, data use and old-OS support in mind. iOS users (~9–12%) are a usable proxy for the affluent segment.
- **Price sensitivity.** The regulator stepped in with EGP 5 and EGP 150 entry bundles right after the price increases. Telecom affordability is politically sensitive, and data cost still shapes behavior at the bottom of the market (favour offline modes and low-data design).

## 5. Digital payments: inclusion, accounts, InstaPay, wallets, Fawry, cash/COD

### Takeaway
- The CBE counts 77.6% financial inclusion at end-2025: 54.7M of 70.5M adults hold an active account, under a broad definition. That is up 219% since 2016.
- There are about 33M mobile wallets (end-Q1 2026), which carried EGP 2.96T of transactions in H1 2026.
- InstaPay had about 12M users and EGP 2.9T of transfers in 2024. It added a 0.1% fee in April 2025 and turned profitable without losing users.
- Fawry processed EGP 586.9B in H1 2026 (+52%), earning about 0.9% of processed value as revenue at a 56.7% EBITDA margin.

Person-to-person digital money movement is mainstream. Online merchant checkout still leans on cash on delivery: about 45% of e-commerce payments in 2024, by a commercial estimate.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Funnel (orders of magnitude, 2024–2026).** Treat each step as a separate estimate, not a strict subset.

  - 54.7M "financially included" under the broad CBE definition; 15.8M adults have no active account of any kind
  - about 33M mobile wallets, about 47% of adults if each person had one (duplicates exist)
  - at least 12M InstaPay users, about 17% of adults at end-2024 and probably higher by 2026
  - online card payers: unknown, probably far fewer
- **Definitions.** The gap between the CBE's 77.6% and the World Bank Findex ([UNVERIFIED]: about 27% account ownership in 2021) reflects definitions. The CBE index counts wallets, prepaid (Meeza) cards and postal accounts ([UNVERIFIED]). Do not read 77.6% as "77.6% can pay online".
- **Ticket size.** InstaPay's average transaction in 2024 was about EGP 1,900 (2.9T ÷ 1.5B), so it is a mass person-to-person rail used for everyday and mid-sized payments.
- **Fees are tolerated.** InstaPay's 0.1% fee caused no user loss, and Fawry earns about 0.89% revenue on processed value (5,222.2M ÷ 586.9B). Small, transparent per-transaction fees on high-utility flows are accepted in Egypt.
- **Where Fawry's growth is.** Classic consumer bill-pay (+2.9%) is mature. Growth comes from B2B2C banking services and embedded finance: lending, neobank and prepaid cards. Banking and Financial Services together make up about 72% of Fawry's revenue.
- **Wallets vs checkout.** Wallet throughput is about EGP 6T annualized (2.96T in H1 2026 ≈ $58B), yet cash on delivery still dominates e-commerce checkout. That leaves a gap at checkout: wallet, InstaPay-link and QR payment at the point of sale, plus COD reconciliation tools for sellers.

## 7. Willingness to pay and monetization models

### Takeaway
Hard evidence on willingness to pay is thin but points one way:
- Mass-market digital prices are in the tens to low hundreds of EGP per month ($1–5). Netflix charges EGP 70–270/month depending on the source and date.
- The regulator pushed telecom entry bundles down to EGP 5 (mobile) and EGP 150 (fixed).
- The clearest proof of monetization comes from transaction fees and B2B2C models. InstaPay's 0.1% fee made it profitable without losing users. Fawry runs a 56.7% EBITDA margin, with about 72% of revenue from banking and financial services.

App-store spending, other subscription benchmarks and convenience-fee data were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- **Netflix Egypt.** Sources conflict:
  - The official help page, as indexed by search, lists Basic EGP 70, Standard EGP 120 and Premium EGP 165 per month — [Netflix Help Center](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/24926/eg)
  - A 2026 listing gives Basic EGP 110, Standard EGP 190 and Premium EGP 270 — [Al Banknote](https://invest.albanknote.com/netflix-subscription-price-egypt)
  - This is unresolved; the help-page snippet may be stale.
- Netflix earlier cut Egyptian prices by about 30%: Basic from EGP 100 to 70, Standard from 165 to 120, Premium from 200 to 165 — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Buzz/Netflix-Slashes-Prices-for-Subscribes-in-Egypt). Egyptian users can pay Netflix in local currency (date not captured) — [Amwal Al Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=102362)
- InstaPay's 0.1% fee from Apr 2025 brought profitability about a year later with no significant decline in adoption — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/north-africa/egypt-instapay-turns-profitable-a-year-after-transfer-fee-rollout-rt18pyrt)
- Fawry, H1 2026: Banking Services 39.6% of revenue (+43.2%), Financial Services 32.3% (+65.1%), consumer bill-pay and top-up (ADP) 19.1% (+2.9%); EBITDA margin 56.7% — [Zawya press release](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/fawry-releases-1h2026-results-435216)
- Telecom prices rose 9–15% in May 2026, and the regulator mandated EGP 5 mobile and EGP 150 fixed entry bundles — [Masrawy](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_economy/details/2026/5/6/2983762)

### Inferences
- **Price points in USD (at ~50.6):** EGP 50 ≈ $1.0, EGP 100 ≈ $2.0, EGP 270 ≈ $5.3. Netflix prices Egypt at about $1.4–5.3/month.
  - A mass-market consumer subscription above about EGP 100–150/month is a premium product.
  - Broad willingness to pay probably sits at EGP 20–100/month, or in per-use micro-fees.
  - If the EGP 110/190/270 listing is current, Netflix raised Egyptian prices by about 57–64% from the 70/120/165 tier. USD-cost services do pass devaluation through to consumers.
- **Monetization models, ranked by the evidence retrieved:**
  1. **Small per-transaction fees on frequent, high-utility flows.** Proven: InstaPay 0.1% with no user loss; Fawry about 0.9% revenue on processed value.
  2. **B2B2C through banks and financial institutions.** Proven: Fawry Banking Services is the largest revenue line at 39.6%.
  3. **Embedded finance (lending, neobank, prepaid cards).** The fastest-growing line (+65%; prepaid cards +116%), but capital-intensive with policy rates at 19–20%. Partner with a licensed lender rather than carry the risk.
  4. **Low-priced, EGP-denominated subscriptions.** These work for entertainment (Netflix in EGP). Expect pressure to keep prices low, and reprice with inflation of about 13–15%.
  5. **Consumer bill-pay convenience fees.** A mature, slow-growth segment (Fawry ADP +2.9%).
  6. **Advertising-only.** Uncertain. Reach is about 52M social identities, and no data was retrieved on Egyptian CPMs or ad budgets.
- **Distribution channels.** Regulators act to protect telecom affordability, and wallets (~33M) and InstaPay (≥12M users) are already widespread. Telco-, wallet- and bank-embedded distribution is plausible for reaching the mass market. This is not verified with carrier-billing data.
- **FX and margin risk.** A product priced in EGP with USD costs (AI inference, cloud) lost about 6–7% of margin overnight in Mar 2026. Build FX buffers into pricing.
- **Sizing anchors (2025–2026), compiled from §1–§5:**
  - residents 109M; adults 70.5M
  - internet users about 90–98M; social identities 51.6M; mobile connections 121M; Android about 88–91%
  - financially included adults 54.7M; mobile wallets about 33M; InstaPay users at least 12M (end-2024)
  - InstaPay EGP 2.9T (2024); wallets EGP 2.96T (H1 2026); Fawry EGP 586.9B (H1 2026)
  - minimum wage EGP 8,000 (≈$158) from Jul 2026
  - exchange rate about EGP 50.6–51 per dollar (Jul–Aug 2026)
  - inflation 12.7% nationwide / 14.5% urban (Aug 2026)
  - policy rate 19%/20% (since Feb 2026)

- Convenience fees were not retrieved:
