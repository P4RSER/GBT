> **Method note (research date 2026-10-03).** Every figure below comes from Arabic/English news reports of official releases (Cabinet complaints system, Consumer Protection Agency, NTRA, CBE, FRA) and from survey publishers. I read them through web-search result snippets and headlines. The network proxy blocked full-page fetching on every domain tried (youm7, masrawy, dostor, ahram, sis.gov.eg, elbalad, mobtada, almalnews, amwalalghad, arabbarometer.org, afrobarometer.org, thenationalnews, reddit, wikipedia). The session's shared web-search quota then ran out partway through, so **search-trend and social-media signals (Q5) could not be collected**. "(snippet)" means the number came from a search engine's summary of the cited article. "(headline)" means it is visible only in the article headline. Unless stated otherwise, Cabinet-system counts are "complaints, requests and inquiries" (شكوى وطلب واستفسار), not complaints alone. Resolution rates are self-reported by the agencies.

## 1. Cabinet Unified Governmental Complaints System (منظومة الشكاوى الحكومية الموحدة): top sectors, complaint types, counts and trends

### Takeaway
The system handled about **2.1 million complaints/requests in 2025, up 17% on 2024**. Monthly volumes in mid-2026 run about 18–31% above the 2025 monthly average: **206k in May, 229k in July and 224k in August 2026**. **Housing & utilities** (water cuts or weak pressure, sewage, housing units) is the #1 sector every period, at about 16–18% of volume. It is followed by Interior/security requests, health, education, environment and electricity. Electricity and health complaints were sharply higher in summer 2026.

### Inferences
- **Implied 2024 total** ≈ 1.8M (2.1M ÷ 1.17).
- **2025 monthly average** ≈ 175k. 2026 months ran above it: **May +18%, July +31%, August +28%** (December 2025 was +2%). Either problems are growing or awareness and use of the system are rising (likely both). If the trend holds, 2026 could total about 2.5M; this rests on only three monthly data points, so confidence is low.
- **Sector shares of 2025 volume:** housing & utilities ≈ 18%, Interior ≈ 9%, health ≈ 7%, education ≈ 6%, environment ≈ 4%, electricity ≈ 4%. Together the six named sectors are ≈ 47%. The remaining ≈ 53% (local development, transport/traffic, supply, social solidarity, petroleum, labor, etc.) was not broken out in the retrieved material.
- **Housing & utilities is structurally #1:** ≈ 16% of monthly volume in May 2026 (33.2k/206k) and July 2026 (36k/229k), against ≈ 31k/month average in 2025, i.e. +7% (May) and +16% (July).
- **Electricity rises in summer, and 2026 looks worse:** +19% from May (10.7k) to August (12.7k) 2026. August 2026 is ≈ 1.9× the 2025 monthly average (≈ 6.8k), *if* the 82k annual figure is right. The cause (billing, prepaid meters, outages) is not identified in the snippets.
- **Health:** August 2026 (16.4k) is ≈ +32% vs the 2025 monthly average (≈ 12.4k). About **1 in 4** health contacts in August 2026 was an urgent distress case (4,076/16.4k), and ≈ 14% concerned state-funded treatment paperwork (2,237). Navigating medical bureaucracy is a recurring, quantifiable friction.
- **Ration cards** make up ≈ 67% of Supply-ministry contacts (4,562/6,824, August 2026).
- **Water:** at least ≈ 5.3k water-cut, weak-pressure or sewage complaints were *resolved* in May 2026 alone, before the summer peak.
- **Bias warning:** these channels over-represent government-delivered services (utilities, health, documents). Price and income problems, which surveys rank #1 (section 4), barely appear. Self-reported resolution rates (92–98%) should not be read as satisfaction. Ministries also run parallel hotlines, and most aggrieved citizens never file, so the totals are a lower bound.

## 2. Consumer Protection Agency (جهاز حماية المستهلك): top complaint categories and counts

### Takeaway
The CPA received **211,732 complaints in 2025** and reports 96% resolved. The largest identifiable categories are:
- **e-commerce/online selling, ~35k**, much of it fraud by anonymous social-media pages, which the CPA calls the biggest consumer risk;

### Inferences
- **NTRA 2025 total = 263,262.** H2 vs H1: total +12.6%, **fixed internet +31.7%**, devices +29.2%, mobile +7.6%, fixed phone −1.9%. Frustration with home internet is rising fastest. The complaint *type* (speed, quota exhaustion, outages, billing) is not confirmed.
- **Implied mobile subscription base ≈ 123 million** (64,099 ÷ 52 per 100k). Mobile is near-universal, so even regulator-level complaints (which come only after the operator fails) understate everyday friction.
- The e& fixed-internet complaint rate is ≈ 6.5× WE's (1,286 vs 198 per 100k). WE takes longest to respond (1.7 days).
- **Egyptians complain by voice:** 88% of NTRA complaints come by call, only ~8% via app or WhatsApp. Consumer products that handle complaints should be WhatsApp/voice-first, not app-only.
- **CBE ≈ 40k complaints a year**, annualized from December 2025 and assuming that month is typical. The themes (debt, card disputes, interest, fees) fit a **fee/charge transparency tool**, e.g. parsing bank SMS alerts to flag fees, interest and duplicate charges, plus a dispute-letter assistant.
- **Insurance complaints are niche** (~1.5k/yr, +4.9% y/y). Low insurance penetration probably suppresses the number, but it is not a mass-market complaint.
- The FRA's new disclosure rules suggest regulator concern about consumer-finance pricing, and could become a data source for instalment/BNPL cost comparison.

## 4. Surveys: the top problems facing Egyptians and their households

### Takeaway
Economic strain dominates. In Arab Barometer Wave IX (fieldwork September 2025), **about 60% of Egyptians said they could not afford enough food in the past month**, including **38% of university graduates**, and **59% worry about having enough food**. Government performance gets its worst ratings on **keeping prices down (85% "badly")** and **creating jobs (78%)**. Most people are dissatisfied with **housing (68%)** and **health care and education (58% each)**; the source attribution of these last figures needs verification. No recent Baseera, Ipsos or YouGov "top problems" poll was found.

### Inferences
- **Cost of living and food affordability is the #1 everyday frustration by breadth.** It affects a majority of households, including the educated middle class (38% of graduates food-insecure). Mass-market money-saving and budgeting tools therefore have a very large base, but willingness to pay is low. Freemium, B2B2C (banks, telcos, retailers) or commission models fit better than subscriptions.
- **The survey ranking of public services matches the complaint-volume ranking:** housing is worst-rated (68%) and is the #1 complaint sector; health and education (58%) are complaint sectors #3 and #4; trash collection (46%) lines up with environment complaints (83k/yr). This convergence makes the housing/utilities, health and education signals robust.
- Inflation keeps hitting discretionary and seasonal spending (+24–27% y/y on seasonal sweets per Baseera), so price-comparison and deal-alert products stay relevant.

No Ipsos or YouGov Egypt "top worries" data was found.
- The exact Arab Barometer Egypt "most important challenge" percentages (Waves VIII/IX) were not retrieved, nor were survey data on **corruption/bureaucracy, traffic, or migration intent**.
- Afrobarometer: no confirmed Egypt round after 2015 was found in this session.

- **Formal complaints across the four main channels total roughly 2.6 million a year** (Cabinet ≈ 2.1M + CPA ≈ 212k + NTRA ≈ 263k + CBE ≈ 40k annualized + FRA ≈ 1.5k; not deduplicated). Combined with the steady flow of "how to complain" explainers, this points to demand for a **WhatsApp-first complaint concierge** that picks the right channel, drafts the complaint, files it and tracks it. Validate with Google Trends and user interviews before building.
- Fraud by anonymous online sellers is officially recognized and large (≈ 35k formal complaints in 2025, probably a small fraction of incidents). That supports trust-and-safety products for social commerce. Whether "fake InstaPay transfer screenshots" are a major sub-type is **unverified** (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- **Untested sources** (all in scope, none collected): Google Trends Egypt; Reddit threads; Facebook groups (e.g., medicine-finding groups, technician-recommendation groups, neighborhood groups); X/Twitter; TikTok/YouTube; newspaper "complaints" columns.
- **Unverified leads from researcher background knowledge. Do not cite as fact; each needs a source before use:**
  - 2023–2024 electricity load-shedding (تخفيف الأحمال) of roughly 1–3 hours a day, reportedly ended mid-2024;
  - localized summer power and water outages since then (e.g., a reported multi-day Giza outage in July 2025);
  - severe medicine shortages in 2024 tied to the FX crisis, with people searching for alternatives and using Facebook "find-my-medicine" groups;
  - scams in which buyers or sellers send **fake InstaPay transfer screenshots**;
  - high household spending on **private tutoring** (CAPMAS HIECS data);
  - the **imported mobile-phone registration/customs regime from January 2025**, which may explain part of the ~25k CPA phone complaints;
  - frequent searches for **gold prices, exchange rates, electricity-bill and traffic-fine inquiries**;
  - **rental broker (سمسار) scams** and fake listings.

## 6. Pain-point profiles (reach, frequency, cost, workaround, existing apps, software solvability) and consolidated ranking

### Takeaway
Weighing breadth (surveys) first and complaint volume second, the most widespread everyday frustrations are:
1. cost of living and food affordability;
2. housing and municipal water/sanitation services;
3. health care access, quality and paperwork;
4. dealing with government procedures and documents;
5. education quality and cost;
6. buying goods: after-sales failures and online-seller fraud;
7. mobile and home-internet quality;
8. electricity bills, meters and outages.

**Software has the clearest leverage on #6** (trust, escrow, warranty, repair). It has partial leverage on #1 (budgeting, price comparison, savings circles, finance-cost transparency), #3 (finding medicine or doctors, paperwork navigation), #4 (reminders, checklists, concierge), and #7/#8 (usage monitoring, prepaid-meter management, outage alerts), plus a cross-cutting complaint-concierge opportunity. **#2 (water and sewage supply) and macro prices are largely structural**; software can only coordinate around them.

**Profile notes for the top pain points**
- **People affected:**
  - Surveys suggest majorities for #1 (≈ 60% food-insecure), #2 (68%* dissatisfied with housing), #3 and #5 (58%*).
  - Telecom (#7) touches nearly everyone (≈ 123M mobile subscriptions).
  - Formal complaint counts (tens to hundreds of thousands) are small fractions of the affected population and should be read as intensity signals, not prevalence.
- **Frequency:** cost of living and internet are daily. Utilities and electricity are monthly, with summer spikes (electricity +19% May→Aug 2026). Health, documents and consumer disputes are episodic but high-stakes.
- **Time/money cost:** almost no quantified data was retrieved. The only measured figures are agency response times (NTRA average 0.86 days; WE fixed internet 1.7 days) and price inflation signals (Baseera +24–27% y/y on seasonal sweets). See Gaps.
- **Software vs structural:**
  - Structural, where software only coordinates around the problem: macro prices and income, water and sewage supply, hospital capacity, electricity generation.
  - Software-solvable at the margin, where small teams can realistically win: trust and verification in social commerce, after-sales and warranty management, paperwork navigation (state-funded treatment, documents), price and fee transparency (groceries, bank fees, consumer-finance cost), usage monitoring (internet quota, prepaid electricity), and complaint routing.
- **Go-to-market implication:** complaint behavior is voice- and phone-first (88% of NTRA complaints by call). Products should run over WhatsApp, voice notes or chatbots rather than needing app downloads. Low willingness to pay points to B2B2C (banks, telcos, retailers, insurers), transaction fees (escrow, delivery, concierge) or freemium.
