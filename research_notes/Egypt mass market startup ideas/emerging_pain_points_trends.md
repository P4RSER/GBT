## 1. Housing and law: old-rent law, new family (personal status) law, new labor law, real-estate tax/registration, traffic rules

### Takeaway
The old-rent overhaul (Law 164 of 2025) is the largest legal change hitting ordinary households. It affects about 1.64m tenant households (2017 census), mostly in Greater Cairo and Alexandria. Rents were reset to 10–20x old levels in 2025, a compulsory 15% yearly increase starts in September 2026, and contracts end around 2030 (shops) and 2032 (homes). Yet only about 100–105k tenants had applied for state "alternative housing" by mid-2026, and that deadline was extended to **12 Oct 2026**. A 355-article family law is before parliament and tops the agenda of the session opening in early October 2026. The new labor law has been in force since September 2025. Cabinet approved tougher traffic fines in December 2025, but the government withdrew them in February 2026 so it could rewrite the whole law.

### Cited Findings

**Old-rent law (Law 164/2025). Status: in force**
- The trigger: on 9 Nov 2024 the Supreme Constitutional Court ruled that fixed rents under Law 136/1981 were unconstitutional, because rent control has to be temporary and reviewed — [ADSERO](https://adsero.me/egypt-enacts-major-reform-to-old-rental-laws/); [TIMEP](https://timep.org/2025/07/10/millions-at-risk-of-eviction-as-egypt-overhauls-rent-laws/)
- Law 164/2025 was ratified and published in the Official Gazette on **4 Aug 2025**. It covers leases signed before 31 Jan 1996; leases from 31 Jan 1996 onward are not covered — [Elwatan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/8113396); [Amwal Al Ghad](https://amwalalghad.com/2025/08/04/تفاصيل-قانون-الإيجار-القديم-الجديد-2025-ب/)
- Transition periods: residential leases are freed after **7 years** (so around Aug 2032) and non-residential leases after **5 years** (around Aug 2030) — [Elwatan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/8113396); [Egypt Independent](https://www.egyptindependent.com/egypt-mandates-15-increase-to-deregulate-old-rent-leases/)
- How rents were reset, by zone: "premium" zones 20x the old rent with a floor of EGP 1,000/month; "medium" zones 10x with a floor of EGP 400; "economic" zones 10x with a floor of EGP 250 — [Egypt Telegraph](https://www.egypttelegraph.com/article/200678/بعد-انتهاء-الحصر،-كيفية-معرفة-تصنيف-مناطق); [ADSERO](https://adsero.me/egypt-enacts-major-reform-to-old-rental-laws/)
- Until their zone was classified, tenants paid a provisional EGP 250/month — [Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5478814.aspx) (search extract). Governorate inventory committees then finished the classification, and the results were published in the Official Gazette and through local units as the official reference for increases — [Egypt Telegraph](https://www.egypttelegraph.com/article/200678/بعد-انتهاء-الحصر،-كيفية-معرفة-تصنيف-مناطق)
- A **mandatory 15% annual rent increase starts in September 2026** and repeats each year through the transition — [Egypt Independent](https://www.egyptindependent.com/egypt-mandates-15-increase-to-deregulate-old-rent-leases/); [Egypt Daily News](https://egyptdailynews.com/annual-old-rent-increase-begins-september-1/)
- Scale: **1.642m households** lived in old-rent units at the 2017 census (7% of households). 82% are in Cairo, Giza, Alexandria and Qalyubia, and Cairo alone holds 41% — [Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5240877.aspx)
- 36% of old-rent households paid under EGP 50/month before the reform (head of CAPMAS) — [Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5166041.aspx)
- A documented database covers about 3m old-rent units, residential and non-residential — [Akhbar El Yom](https://akhbarelyom.com/news/newdetails/4620744/1/قاعدة-بيانات-موثقة-3-ملايين-وحدة-إيجار-ق). About 300k old-rent flats were closed (vacant) — [Egypt Telegraph](https://www.egypttelegraph.com/article/142406/رئيس-التعبئة-والإحصاء-يكشف-سبب-إغلاق-300)
- Alternative housing: applications opened online (Digital Egypt platform) and at post offices nationwide on **1 Oct 2025**. Applicants pick a category, give the property address, choose an allocation system (subsidised rent, rent-to-own, or ownership through mortgage finance) and a preferred area, and can open an account to track status — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsContent/1/2/551077/Egypt/Society/-Old-rent-tenants-to-apply-for-alternative-housing.aspx); [Official Real Estate Platform blog](https://blogs.realestate.gov.eg/news/housing-ministry-explains-how-to-apply-for-old-rent-replacement-apartments-step-by-step/); [Egypt Independent](https://www.egyptindependent.com/egypt-launches-form-for-alternatives-housing-to-those-affected-by-old-rent-law/)
- Who can apply: the original residential tenant, the spouse if the lease passed to them, or any tenant the lease was later extended to. For non-residential units, the original tenant or the leaseholder after extension. The government says applying does not mean leaving: nobody will be asked to vacate until they have actually been given an alternative unit — [Official Real Estate Platform blog](https://blogs.realestate.gov.eg/news/receiving-applications-from-tenants-eligible-under-the-old-rent-law-to-begin-on-october-1/); [RE Platform deadline explainer](https://blogs.realestate.gov.eg/the-old-rent-law-changes-in-egypt-what-affected-tenants-need-to-know-before-the-deadline/)
- Cabinet extended the application window by three months, to **12 Oct 2026** — [Masrawy, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2026/7/23/3022342/3-%D8%A3%D8%B4%D9%87%D8%B1-%D9%85%D9%87%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B7-%D9%88%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%83%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%8A%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%85/); [Mobtada](https://www.mobtada.com/egypt/1628957/%D8%B3%D9%83%D9%86-%D8%A8%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%8A%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%85-%D9%85%D9%88%D8%B9%D8%AF-%D8%BA%D9%84%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%85-%D9%88%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%B5%D9%8A%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%B1%D8%B2-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B7-%D8%B9%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%85%D8%B5%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%82%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%A9)

### Inferences

**Old-rent law: predicted household pains (2026–2032)**
- **Prediction: confusion over rent arithmetic and proof of payment is the immediate, recurring pain (2026–2028).** Reasoning: rents were reset by zone after a provisional EGP 250 payment, which implies back-pay differences, and a compounding 15% rise now lands every September. Both sides dispute amounts (36% of tenants paid under EGP 50), and tenants need dated, provable payment records to defend against non-payment claims. My calculation: a premium-zone tenant at the EGP 1,000 floor pays about EGP 1,150 from Sep 2026, about 1,323 from Sep 2027, about 1,521 from Sep 2028, and about 2,313 by Sep 2031 (1.15^6 ≈ 2.31). That is still far below Giza's EGP 7,500 market median, so **the real shock is the end of the contract** (Aug 2030 for shops, Aug 2032 for homes).
- **Prediction: the low uptake of alternative housing (about 100–105k applications against about 1.64m households, roughly 6%) means a late second wave of anxiety.** Reasoning: most households are either staying and paying, waiting, or do not understand or trust the process. When allocations, lotteries and handovers begin, applicants must choose between rent, rent-to-own and mortgage, each needing its own affordability calculation and documents. Non-applicants who later regret it will look for any remaining route.
- **Prediction: the people doing the paperwork are often adult children, many of them abroad.** Reasoning: old-rent tenancies date from before 1996, so tenants skew elderly, while the process runs through Digital Egypt or post offices. This links to the concierge and diaspora parent-care ideas below.
- **Prediction: a relocation wave in 2028–2032.** Hundreds of thousands of households, plus many small shops facing the 2030 deadline, will need to search for, finance and move to new homes or premises, concentrated in Cairo, Giza and Alexandria. They will also need to negotiate buy-outs or new leases with landlords.
- **Software idea, an "old-rent companion":** a rent calculator by Gazette zone; reminders for the 15% increase; rent payment through InstaPay or Fawry with time-stamped receipts that can be used in disputes; legal-notice templates (warning notices, court deposit of rent); a tracker for alternative-housing applications; and a vetted lawyer marketplace. From 2028 it would add a "relocation planner" covering affordable rentals, movers and mortgage pre-qualification.
- **Monetisation:** lawyer and notary referral fees; rent-collection SaaS for landlords with several old-rent units; a paid assisted-application service; and, from 2028, relocation lead fees from developers, brokers, movers and mortgage banks.
- **Existing players:** Haqq.ai (legal AI content), Landager (compliance), the government platform itself, and property portals (Property Finder, Aqarmap, Nawy, from background knowledge). I found no product focused on the old-rent transition.
- **Risks:** the constitutional challenge (outcome unknown) and the political sensitivity of the issue.

**Family law: predicted pains (if passed in the 2026/27 session)**
- **Prediction: a burst of demand for information and logistics among separated parents.** Reasoning: about 274k divorces a year (2024), plus all existing separated parents, would need to learn the new custody age (15, then the child chooses), the father's new place second in line, visitation and "hosting" rules, and how to claim alimony through the new Family Support Fund. Custody and alimony fights turn on evidence: proof of expenses and of attended or missed visits.
- **Software idea:** an Egyptian-Arabic co-parenting app with a shared visitation/hosting calendar and check-ins, a child-expense ledger with receipts, secure messaging with a tone filter, an alimony-fund application tracker, and an AI explainer of the new law that hands off to a lawyer.
- **Monetisation:** per-parent subscription (my guess: EGP 50–100/month), lawyer referrals, and paid court-ready PDF reports.
- **Existing players:** I found no localised product. Global apps such as OurFamilyWizard (background knowledge) are not in Arabic or adapted to Egyptian law.
- **Timing risk:** the law's content may still change, so build the law-neutral logistics first.

**Labor law: low mass-market priority**
- **Prediction:** the pain falls mostly on employers (B2B HR and payroll compliance with the 3% raise, leave tiers and remote-work contracts). A free employee "rights calculator" could serve as a lead magnet but is not a standalone mass-market business.

**Real-estate tax: low priority**
- **Prediction:** if passed, the higher exemption reduces the burden on most main homes, so it creates little new household pain.

**Traffic: medium-low until the new law passes**
- **Prediction:** cabinet has already signalled much higher fines, so a comprehensive traffic law is likely in 2026–2027. Combined with camera enforcement, drivers will want alerts about fines, help disputing them, and reminders for licence renewal and insurance. A "car-ownership companion" could earn from insurance and maintenance leads. Wait for the law's final text.

## 2. Prices, subsidies and the 2026 energy shock: fuel, electricity, the shift from in-kind to cash subsidy, medicine, school and transport prices, gold

### Takeaway
2026 brought a **war-driven energy shock**. The Israel–Iran war that began on 28 Feb 2026 cut Israeli gas exports to Egypt, oil reached about $108 and the pound briefly passed EGP 52–54/$. That broke the October 2025 one-year fuel-price freeze: fuel rose EGP 3/litre on 10 Mar 2026 and metro fares rose on 27 Mar. Electricity went up for heavy users in April and for households on average (+12%) in August 2026. Urban inflation was 14.5% in August 2026. Under the IMF program (7th review completed 30 Jul 2026), cost-recovery pricing continues and automatic fuel indexation was due to resume at end-Q2 2026. ### Inferences
- **Prediction, "price risk moves to households" (the cash-subsidy shift, FY2026/27):** about 15–17m households (my estimate: 61m beneficiaries divided by about 3.5–4 people per household) will stop receiving fixed-price goods and start receiving a fixed cash or e-balance. **Inflation risk (14.5% in Aug 2026) passes from the state to the family.** The new tasks: stretch a fixed sum across the month, compare grocers' prices (no longer fixed), decide whether to buy bread at market price, check whether you are still eligible during the "cleanup", and file appeals. Reasoning: every in-kind-to-cash switch adds budgeting and price-search work. The weak uptake in Port Said signals confusion, and scammers will use "update your card data" messages as bait (see Section 6).
  - **Software idea:** a "subsidy wallet companion", voice-first and in Egyptian Arabic, on WhatsApp and a lightweight app. It would offer balance and eligibility checks, an appeals guide, price comparison for the subsidy basket across nearby grocers, and scam alerts. For grocers (the ration-card retailers), a simple ledger, POS-lite and loyalty tool for unified-card spenders.
  - **Monetisation:** once the subsidy becomes spendable money, merchants compete for it (about EGP 36bn a year in goods, plus possibly part of the EGP 124bn bread budget). That supports FMCG-sponsored offers aimed at cardholders, merchant SaaS fees, and aggregated (privacy-safe) price and demand insights.
  - **Existing players:** the state, Egypt Post and the unified card own the rails. Fawry, Aman and Masary agent networks exist (background knowledge). I found no consumer companion product.
  - **Risks:** the card's design is unknown (closed-loop at authorised grocers or open-loop), timelines may slip, and revenue per user is low.
- **Prediction, electricity bill shock and tier management:** two tariff rises in 2026, plus record heat (Section 7) and the IMF cost-recovery path, mean households will try to forecast their monthly kWh, avoid jumping a tariff tier, and avoid running out of prepaid credit. Middle-class families will weigh inverter ACs and rooftop solar, which vendors say pays back in under 5 years. Reasoning: under tiered tariffs, tier jumps cause bill shock, and the state still covers 58–75% of cost for low tiers, so more rises are likely in 2027–2028.
  - **Software idea:** a "home energy companion": photograph the meter (or enter the reading) for a tier forecast, a daily kWh budget, prepaid low-balance alerts, AC usage advice, and a solar or inverter ROI calculator. **Monetisation:** solar installer and inverter-AC retailer leads, instalment (BNPL) financing referrals, and ads.
- **Prediction, more frequent fuel price changes:** with indexation resumed (quarterly committee reviews), drivers, especially ride-hailing and delivery drivers, will need cost tracking. The economics of converting a car to natural gas also changed after the 30% CNG rise (see the gig-driver idea in Section 5).
- **Prediction, medicine "repricing moments" every 6 months under Decision 480/2026:** consumers will look for generic equivalents, stock up before review dates and compare pharmacies, especially families managing chronic medicine for elderly parents. A price-history, generic-finder and refill-reminder app could earn pharmacy and delivery commissions. The market is crowded: Chefaa, Yodawy and Vezeeta, from background knowledge.
- **Prediction, school fees up 20–40%:** demand for school-fee instalment financing and cheaper substitutes for private lessons (AI tutoring), which links to the Baccalaureate idea in Section 3.
- **Prediction, gold:** gold is the default household store of value, and high entry prices plus volatility (−3% in September) feed demand for small-ticket gold savings and "fair price including workmanship" calculators. A 20g engagement set (shabka) is about EGP 122k at EGP 6,100/g, which also bears on marriage costs. Crowded: eDahab, iSagha and licensed fractional-gold apps.
- **Prediction, transport:** fare rises together with new lines (Section 4) create demand for fare-aware trip planning. Monetisation is weak.

## 3. Government digitization: digital ID and e-payments, the Universal Health Insurance rollout, the Egyptian Baccalaureate, smart/prepaid meters, phone customs

### Takeaway
State services are moving to digital-first channels at the same moment several new processes launched for ordinary people: old-rent alternative-housing applications (Digital Egypt plus post offices), the unified subsidy card, Universal Health Insurance phase 2 (about 12.8m people, live in Minya since spring 2026), the Egyptian Baccalaureate (92% uptake, paid resits, a rule requiring 60% of assessments to be done), the end of the travellers' phone-customs exemption (21 Jan 2026, via the "Telephony" app), prepaid "coded" electricity meters (about 2.3m installed), online ID renewal and a Digital Identity platform. Each adds tasks for households with limited digital skills.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Prediction, a surge of deadline-driven digital paperwork in 2025–2027:** at least six new or newly digital processes overlap: alternative-housing applications (deadline 12 Oct 2026), the unified card and cash-subsidy appeals (FY2026/27), UHI registration in phase-2 governorates, the Telephony phone tax for diaspora visitors, Baccalaureate resit fees (July 2027) and coded-meter applications. The people most affected (elderly old-rent tenants, low-income subsidy households, Minya and the rural Delta) have the weakest digital skills. Reasoning: offline "service offices" (مكاتب خدمات) already charge to fill in government forms, and this demand grows with each new digital process. It can move to a WhatsApp concierge with doorstep collection and delivery of documents, the same model as the passport-photo example.
  - **Software idea:** a WhatsApp-first "digital service office" with checklists per process, document scanning, e-payment, status tracking and a courier for printed documents. **Monetisation:** a fee per service (my guess: EGP 50–300), with diaspora relatives paying for their parents' paperwork, plus B2B deals with employers and unions. **Risks:** handling users' credentials (some steps need a power of attorney), never presenting itself as the government, and compliance with Egypt's personal-data protection law.
- **Prediction, UHI phase 2:** 12.8m newly covered people must register family files, choose facilities, understand co-payments and contributions, and follow referrals. The need is real, but consumers will resist paying to navigate a public service. Best options: bundle navigation into the concierge or parent-care products, or sell B2B to private providers contracting with UHI.
- **Prediction, new parent tasks under the Baccalaureate:**
  - tracking the 60% assessment completion per subject, which is now a risk to exam eligibility;
  - deciding whether paid resits (EGP 200 per subject) are worth it, given that the highest mark counts;
  - choosing a track that leads to the desired faculty.
  The **first university admission round under the new system is summer 2028**, so there will be an information vacuum with no historical cut-off scores. Reasoning: new rules plus high stakes plus no precedent produce anxious parents willing to pay, as Egypt's private-tutoring spending shows.
  - **Software idea:** a planner app (assessment tracker, resit optimiser, track-to-faculty mapper) plus an Egyptian-Arabic AI tutor aligned to the new curricula. **Monetisation:** family subscriptions and licences for schools or tutoring centres.
- **Prediction, phone customs:** diaspora visitors and anyone bringing phones as gifts now face Telephony registration and tax deadlines (90 days). The pain is sharp but narrow; fold it into a concierge or diaspora product.
- **Prediction, prepaid and smart meters:** running out of credit, friction topping up, confusion reading the meter, and billing disputes. This feeds the energy companion in Section 2.

### Gaps
- Adoption numbers for the Digital Identity platform, and whether a chip-based national ID is planned. Not verified.
- UHI contribution rates for informal workers. Not verified this round.
- Size of the Baccalaureate cohort. Not found; from background knowledge, roughly 700–800k students per secondary cohort (unverified). Also not found: university admission rules for Baccalaureate holders and the timeline to retire the old Thanaweya Amma.
- The smart-meter rollout timeline beyond the pilots.

## 4. Urban change: new cities, long commutes, new transit lines, missing services

### Takeaway
The East Nile monorail (New Cairo ↔ New Administrative Capital, 16 stations, open 6am–6pm, EGP 20–80 by zone) started service in May 2026, adding to the LRT and metro extensions. About 48k civil servants commuted daily to the New Administrative Capital (2024 data), with employee housing in nearby Badr City. Rents are rising in the new and western cities. ### Inferences
- **Prediction, first/last-mile and after-hours gaps:** the monorail closes at 6pm and its stations sit far from many compounds and employee housing, which leaves evening and shift workers and residents in new districts with gaps. Expect demand for shuttle pooling to stations, employer commute programs, and night-time alternatives. Monetisation could come from B2B contracts with employers and compounds, or per-seat fares.
- **Prediction, multimodal fare complexity:** metro tiers, monorail zones, the LRT, the BRT and microbuses together after the 2026 fare changes make "how much will this trip cost, door to door?" a common question. A trip planner has real but hard-to-monetise demand (ads, B2B), and Google Maps and Swvl are partial incumbents (background knowledge).
- **Prediction, home services in new cities:** growth in the New Administrative Capital, New Cairo, 6th of October, Sheikh Zayed and New Alamein raises demand for trusted maintenance (AC repair at the height of heat waves, water tanks and pumps, solar). This is a trust and marketplace problem with existing players (background knowledge). Medium priority.

## 5. Demographics and society: aging parents and the diaspora, doctor emigration, marriage costs, divorce and custody, youth unemployment and gig work

### Takeaway
Egypt has about 109m residents. The elderly population is growing (9.8m aged 60+, 9.1%) while fertility falls (2.34 children per woman in 2025). More than 10m Egyptians work abroad and sent a record **$47.3bn in FY2025/26**. Doctors are leaving in large numbers: about 120k of 220k syndicate members work abroad. Marriages fell 2.5% and divorces rose 3.1% in 2024 (273,892). Headline unemployment is low (5.8% in Q2 2026), but it is 14.4% for women and 8.8% in cities. Millions of delivery and gig workers lack contracts and insurance, and a draft law to formalise them is under discussion.

### Inferences
- **Prediction, remote care of parents is a large and growing pain that diaspora money can pay for.** Reasoning: four trends converge. The 60+ population is about 9.8m and growing. Doctors are scarce (62% abroad or on leave), which makes appointments and home visits harder to secure. The bureaucracy elderly people face is getting harder (UHI, old-rent transitions, unified card, prepaid meters, drug repricing every 6 months). And more than 10m relatives abroad sent $47.3bn last year. Adult children in the Gulf or the West need a trusted local operator to handle bills, medicines, doctors, paperwork and daily check-ins.
  - **Software idea:** a "remote parent care" subscription with a dashboard for the child abroad, plus a local operations layer: weekly check-in calls, medicine refills and delivery, electricity top-ups and bill payment, doctor or nurse home visits through partners, paperwork through the concierge, and an emergency line. One angle: Egyptian doctors abroad could provide Arabic tele-consultations, making the diaspora both the paying customer and part of the supply.
  - **Monetisation:** hard-currency subscriptions (my guess: $25–80/month), per-visit fees and partner commissions.
  - **Existing players:** home-care agencies and telemedicine apps such as Vezeeta (background knowledge). I found no diaspora-paid, all-in-one product.
- **Prediction, marriage costs:** with 21K gold at about EGP 6,100/g, the engagement gold (shabka) and the household furnishings (gehaz) become multi-year savings projects, which helps explain falling marriage numbers. Digital rotating savings circles (gam'eya; MoneyFellows, from background knowledge) and gold-savings plans fit here. The space is crowded and was not pursued further.
- **Prediction, the logistics of divorce:** covered under the family law in Section 1 (co-parenting app).

**Why each ranks where it does (predictions with reasoning)**
1. **Digital service office.** This is the broadest "why now": at least six new digital or semi-digital processes land within about 18 months on people with weak digital skills. It is also the cheapest to start (WhatsApp, human agents, checklists), and there is proven willingness to pay, since offline service offices already charge for this. It is a base layer for #3, #5 and #9 and can reuse the passport-photo playbook of printing and delivery. Risks: legal handling of credentials; never looking like an official government channel.
2. **Energy companion.** Nearly every household has an electricity meter, and tariffs are on an announced cost-recovery path, so bill shock keeps coming. The money comes from the supply side (solar, inverter ACs, financing), not from cash-strapped consumers. The minimum product (meter-photo OCR or manual readings plus a tariff model) needs no government API.
3. **Diaspora parent care.** Relatives abroad pay in hard currency and are used to paying for convenience. Demand is pushed up by the doctor exodus, aging and new bureaucracy (including elderly old-rent tenants). It is operations-heavy, so start with one city (Greater Cairo) and a narrow bundle: bills, medicine and a weekly check-in.
4. **Baccalaureate.** Urgency is extreme (new rules, paid resits, no precedent for university admission in 2028) and Egyptian parents pay for education. It is a crowded content market, so win on strategy tools (the 60% tracker and resit maths) rather than video lessons.
5. **Old-rent.** The deadline-driven pain starts now (the 15% rise, disputes, applications), and the much larger relocation wave builds towards 2030 and 2032, which lets a team build trust early and earn from relocation later. It ranks lower on breadth (about 1.6m tenant households plus landlords, concentrated in Cairo, Giza and Alexandria) and carries legal risk (the pending constitutional ruling).
6. **Scam shield.** The pain is broad and growing and is directly fed by items 1, 5 and 9. Consumers rarely pay for security, so the realistic route is B2B2C with banks and telcos (slow sales) and the technology is constrained on iOS. Strong as a trust feature bundled into #1 and #3.
7. **Co-parenting and family law.** There is little competition and the logistics pain is clear, but timing depends on when the law passes and what it finally says. Build the law-neutral logistics first.
8. **Medicine.** The 6-monthly repricing creates recurring moments of need, but strong incumbents (Chefaa, Yodawy, Vezeeta, from background knowledge) make it a better feature of #3 than a standalone company.
9. **Cash subsidy.** It is the broadest new behaviour change of all (60m+ people), but its design and timing depend on the government and revenue per user is low. Monetisation through merchants and FMCG brands becomes viable only if the balance can be spent at competing merchants. Watch the Port Said pilot closely.
10. **Gig drivers.** Real cost pressure plus a formalisation law in the pipeline, but revenue per user is low and success depends on insurer and fintech partners.
