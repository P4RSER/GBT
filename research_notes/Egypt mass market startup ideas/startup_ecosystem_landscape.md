## 1. Funding: totals and deal counts 2022–2026, sector mix, largest rounds, active investors, and government initiatives (Startup Charter)

### Takeaway
Egyptian startup funding peaked at a record $517M across 160 deals in 2022 (MAGNiTT). It then collapsed in 2023 during the devaluation crisis. By count, deals have kept shrinking since (84 in 2024 per Wamda, 69 VC deals in 2025 per MAGNiTT, about 29 in H1 2026), while dollars have concentrated in a few large fintech, proptech and debt deals. Headline totals vary by up to 2x depending on the tracker and on whether debt is counted ($304M VC per MAGNiTT vs. $614M equity plus debt per the government for 2025). The Startup Charter, launched in February 2026, adds incentives such as tax simplification, certification, and a target to mobilize $1B, but it is not yet visible in the deal data.

### Inferences
- **Deal counts have fallen by more than half since 2022** (160 in 2022 to 69 in 2025, both MAGNiTT; H1 2026 at ~29 points to a similar or lower 2026 pace). Average ticket size has risen: about $3.2M (2022, MAGNiTT), $4.0M (2024, Wamda), $4.4M (2025, MAGNiTT) and $5.5M (H1 2026). Capital is going to fewer, later-stage companies. A small, new team should not count on easy seed money and should plan to get to revenue early.
- **Fintech and e-commerce took about 83% of H1 2026 dollars ($132.5M of $158.9M) in only 10 deals.** The other ~19 deals shared roughly $26M, about $1.4M each. Non-fintech categories are mostly seed-sized.
- **Government totals run about 2x private trackers.** The $614M for 2025 includes debt; it implies roughly $407M for 2024 on the same basis, against Wamda's $334M. The report should quote MAGNiTT or Wamda for VC comparisons and label the government figure "equity plus debt".
- **Egypt's #1 Africa rankings in H1 2025 and H1 2026 rest on a few big deals** (Nawy, valU, Tasaheel, Spiro) and on counting debt. On equity in H1 2026, Nigeria led.
- **Implied exchange rate of about EGP 52/USD in early 2026**: valU's FY2025 revenue of EGP 5.6B was reported as $107M (see section 2). Consumer prices set in EGP translate into far fewer dollars than they would have in 2022, which hurts unit economics for any business with USD costs such as cloud, AI APIs or app-store fees.

## 3. Failures, shutdowns, layoffs and down rounds (2022–2026) and their root causes

### Takeaway
Verified evidence in this session is thin but points to recurring failure modes:
- A macro and currency shock: the pound fell about 40% by mid-2023 and deal counts fell 70% in H1 2023.
- Founders leaving for Saudi Arabia.
- A "fintech bloodbath" in P2P and wallets that forced Telda to pivot twice, first to custody and brokerage, then to credit.
- Failure to find product-market fit in B2B SaaS: TradeHub shut down in 2025, about 18 months after raising $1.4M.

The best-known failures named in the brief (Capiter's collapse, Swvl's restructuring, MaxAB–Wasoko) could not be re-verified this session; see Gaps.

### Inferences
- **Root cause 1, currency.** Devaluation cut dollar revenue per user and caused the 2023 funding drought. Businesses with USD costs (cloud, AI inference, imported hardware) and EGP prices face margin squeeze whenever the pound moves.
- **Root cause 2, commoditized consumer fintech features.** P2P and wallet products got squeezed into a "bloodbath", and survivors moved to licensed credit or brokerage. A small team should avoid consumer fintech built on transfers, wallets or prepaid cards.
- **Root cause 3, no product-market fit in B2B workflow SaaS within the runway.** TradeHub burned a $1.4M pre-seed over about 18 months and two pivots. Selling SMB/B2B SaaS in Egypt takes longer than a typical pre-seed runway unless the product solves an urgent, paid problem.
- **Root cause 4, brain and capital drain to KSA.** Teams and investors favor Gulf markets, which leaves Egypt-only mass-market consumer software comparatively under-funded. That is both a risk (less capital) and possibly an opening (less competition).

  - 5M+ registered users is category-leader territory (Thndr, MoneyFellows).
  - Meaningful revenue ($100M+) has so far come from lending (valU), not from app fees.
  - A small team's realistic year-1–2 targets would be far lower, likely tens of thousands of active users. That is an estimate without a sourced Egyptian benchmark.
- **Average deal size** (total dollars divided by deal count): about $3.2M (2022), $4.0M (2024), $4.4M (2025) and $5.5M (H1 2026). The rising average with fewer deals implies thinner seed activity. The only verified 2024–2026 Egyptian pre-seed figure is TradeHub's $1.4M. There is no sourced basis for a "typical" pre-seed or seed range or valuation, so the report should not state one without new data.
