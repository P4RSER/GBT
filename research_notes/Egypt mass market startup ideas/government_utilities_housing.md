## 1. Government services and documents: volumes, state of digitization, remaining pain, expediters (معقب) and startups

### Takeaway
Digitization is real but still partial. Digital Egypt had about 242–245 services and about 10.4–10.7 million users in 2025, roughly a tenth of the population. It handled 23.8M transactions in 2025 and about 20M in the first half of 2026. By comparison, the civil registry alone reported issuing about 1.2M national ID cards and 1.3M computerized birth certificates in a single month. Some items are now delivered to the door: criminal-record certificates (فيش) and renewed driving licenses. Beyond that, most high-volume documents still go through physical offices, post offices and mobile caravans. Several concierge and "requirements guide" startups have appeared (Govcoo, Baseta, Enkzni), but none showed up as a scaled consumer brand. I found no figure for the size of the informal expediter (معقب) market.

### Inferences
- **Most interactions are still in person.** About 10.4–10.7M Digital Egypt users is roughly 10% of a population of about 108M (the population figure is not verified in this session). The platform's 23.8M transactions in 2025 are small next to civil-registry output: if the reported month of 1.2M IDs plus 1.3M birth certificates is typical, that is about 30M documents a year for these two items alone. The H1-2026 pace (about 20M) suggests online volume is roughly doubling, but from a low base.
- **Citizens request the same documents over and over.** About 1.3M computerized birth certificates a month is far above Egypt's annual births. People keep re-requesting the same document for schools, passports, ration cards and jobs. This points to a "family document vault + reuse + expiry reminders" product.
- **Door-to-door delivery by the state weakens the case for errand concierges on simple items** (فيش, license renewal). The paid opportunity shifts to:
  - complex, multi-visit procedures: الشهر العقاري powers of attorney and sales, vehicle transfers, inheritance (إعلام وراثة), reconciliation of building violations (see the coded-meters finding in §2), legalization and certified translation;
  - Egyptians abroad who need procedures done by proxy;
  - B2B bundles, e.g. HR onboarding packs (فيش, birth certificate, military status), car dealers and real-estate brokers.
- **Concierge startups have struggled before.** Govcoo, Baseta and Enkzni exist(ed) since the 2010s, yet none appears as a scaled brand. That signals operating and monetization difficulty, so a new entrant should lead with software (navigation, checklists, reminders, form pre-fill) and sell human handling as a premium add-on.
- **Requirements change often** (new Shahr Aqari SMS rules, ration-card deletions, tariff and procedure changes). An up-to-date, Egyptian-Arabic "what exactly do I need, where, and how much" assistant is a real gap. The government's own AI chat apps (Karnak Chat) are a competitive risk.
- **Regional split.** Mobile caravans exist because access is harder outside the cities. Rural and Upper-Egypt users likely face more trips and higher time costs (inference; no data found).

## 2. Utilities: prepaid electricity, bill disputes, load-shedding, gas, water, internet and mobile, utility apps

### Takeaway
Electricity is the biggest recurring household touchpoint.
- **Meters and recharging:** there were **22.9M prepaid meters as of 30 Jun 2026**, with about 2.5M more targeted by end-FY2026/27. Owners recharge cards at Fawry, Masary and Aman kiosks, at branches, or with NFC phone apps (Sahl, Kahraba, Fawry).
- **Billing:** in 2026 the ministry ordered that no bill be issued without a real meter photo, which implies estimated bills were a systemic problem. Flat-rate "coded meters" (2.74 EGP/kWh) were raised in Parliament in June 2026.
- **Load-shedding** (2023–mid-2024) has not returned according to officials, including for summer 2026.
- **Tariffs:** household prices rose again on July 2026 consumption, but reports conflict on the scope.

Gas readings are self-reported monthly (by the 27th) through 15 channels, including an OCR app. Home internet (about 13.1M fixed broadband subscriptions, May 2026) saw price rises of 17–30% in late 2024 and 9–15% in May 2026, with complaints about quotas running out.

**Electricity bills, disputes and coded meters (العدادات الكودية)**
- The Ministry of Electricity ordered that **no electricity bill be issued unless backed by a real, documented photo of the meter**. An outsourced specialist company photographs meters and records readings, and inspection teams check that recorded data matches actual use. The goal is to reduce reliance on averages and estimates and cut billing errors, while speeding up the regularization of coded meters — [Banker.news, ~5 Sep 2026](https://banker.news/151244)
- **Coded meters** (installed in buildings without full legal status, also known as "practice" or ممارسة billing) are billed at a **flat 2.74 EGP/kWh**, while legal meters start at subsidized tiers. MPs asked for a review, especially for citizens who had paid assessment fees (رسوم المقايسة), and for a distinction between officially installed coded meters and "practice" meters — [Al Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5518618); House briefing request on unified coded-meter pricing — [Masrawy, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2026/6/15/3003266/غد-ا-مجلس-النواب-يناقش-طلب-إحاطة-بشأن-تسعير-العدادات-الكودية-الموحدة)
- The Senate recommended giving coded-meter holders **an extra year to reconcile** their status — [Egypt Telegraph](https://www.egypttelegraph.com/article/251367/الشيوخ-يوصي-بمنح-أصحاب-العدادات-الكودية-سنة); headline "تثبيت تعريفة العدادات الكودية وإلغاء نظا[م]…" ("coded-meter tariff fixed and … system cancelled"), ~spring 2026 — [Akhbar El Yom](https://m.akhbarelyom.com/news/NewDetails/4808458/1/تثبيت-تعريفة-العدادات-الكودية-وإلغاء-نظا)

Note: these are exactly +20%, which does not fit the 9–15% May 2026 range, so the date of this round is uncertain — [Egypt Today](https://www.egypttoday.com/Article/3/146940/Egypt-introduces-new-affordable-internet-plans-amid-15-telecom-price); [Zawia3](https://zawia3.com/en/internet-speed/)
- Subscribers complain about monthly data caps running out fast as faster fiber raises consumption; headline: "Egypt Raises Internet Prices Up to 15% — While Telecom Profits Surge and Complaints Mount" — [Zawia3](https://zawia3.com/en/internet-speed/)

### Inferences
- **Scale.** 22.9M prepaid electricity meters means tens of millions of households and shops perform a recharge task at least monthly. The 40M-meter replacement plan suggests prepaid will become the majority format. Basic recharging is already solved (kiosks plus NFC apps from Sahl, Kahraba and Fawry). The unsolved part is **visibility**: "how much have I used, which tier am I in, when will the balance run out, why was a lump sum deducted (مستحقات), why won't the card charge." The 2026 tariff confusion (12% average vs top-tier only) and the earlier official denial both show people cannot easily tell what they will pay.
- **Bill disputes are systemic.** The ministry's photo-required billing reform (Sept 2026) confirms estimated bills were common enough to need a national fix. A consumer tool where users photograph their meter monthly gives them an evidence trail for complaints. It could also auto-draft complaints and estimate bills by tier.
- **Coded meters link utilities to building paperwork.** They mark buildings with incomplete legal status. Households pay a flat 2.74 EGP/kWh, versus subsidized tiers starting much lower, until the building regularizes. That gives a strong monetary motive to complete violation reconciliation (التصالح), which is itself a paperwork pain (see §1 and §5). This is likely concentrated in informal and peri-urban areas and in the governorates outside planned districts (inference).
- **A combined "home bills copilot" has the widest reach.** The monthly cadence across electricity recharge, gas reading (by the 27th), water and internet renewal or quota suits one app with reminders, meter-photo OCR, quota and balance forecasts, and payments via licensed aggregators. Monetization is thin on payments (incumbents Fawry, Masary, Aman). More promising: premium features for landlords and owners of multiple meters, plus affiliate lead-gen for energy savings (efficient ACs, solar and inverters) and internet package switching.
- **Load-shedding is currently a weak hook.** Officials say no cuts since mid-2024, including summer 2026. A "power outage tracker" would only matter if cuts return. The 2026 conservation measures show the grid is still tight.
- **Utilities' own apps are fragmented by provider and company.** Electricity has several distribution companies; gas has Petrotrade and others; water is run by governorate companies. Fragmentation is itself a pain an aggregator can address (inference; app ratings not retrieved).

  - An August 2024 tariff hike raised tiers by about 14–50%.
  - Egypt's tier structure means crossing high-consumption thresholds raises the price applied to consumption, a classic "tier-jump" pain.

## 3. Renting: market structure, brokers, listing platforms, trust, contracts, and the 2025 old-rent law

### Takeaway
The only verified data here concerns **old rent (الإيجار القديم)**:
- about **1.8M households (~6M people)**, **93% urban**, and **82% in four governorates (Cairo, Giza, Alexandria, Qalyubia)**;
- **35% pay under EGP 50 a month**;
- the new framework **ends residential old-rent contracts 7 years after the law takes effect** (non-residential after 5);
- one projection has old-rent households falling to **756k by 2027**.

This points to a large, Greater-Cairo- and Alexandria-concentrated wave of households that will need to rent at market prices, buy, or move into state units between now and about 2032. I could not research broker fees, listing platforms, fake listings, deposits, contracts or 2024–2026 rental prices before the search budget ran out.

### Cited Findings
- The old-rent law affects about **1.8M households (~6M individuals)**. It is overwhelmingly urban (**93%**), and **four governorates (Greater Cairo, i.e. Cairo and Giza, plus Alexandria and Qalyubia) account for 82%** of cases. More than **35% of affected households pay under EGP 50/month**. Figures date from mid-2025, after the House's initial approval — [Al-Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5240877.aspx); [Al Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5129045)
- A projection has old-rent households declining to **756,000 by 2027** — [Al-Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5237316.aspx)
- Under the new law, **residential** old-rent contracts end **seven years** after the law takes effect, and **non-residential** contracts after **five years** — [Al-Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5240877.aspx); [Al Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5128066)
- The state set out steps for an old-rent tenant to obtain an **ownership or rental unit** (state housing alternative) under the amendments (7 May 2025, draft stage) — [Masrawy, 7 May 2025](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2025/5/7/2783250/خطوات-حصول-المستأجر-على-شقة-تمليك-أو-إيجار-بعد-تعديلات-الإيجار-القديم-)

### Inferences
- **The old-rent transition creates a long-tail demand wave.** About 1.8M households, concentrated in Cairo, Giza, Alexandria and Qalyubia and often elderly or low-rent (35% under EGP 50), must absorb rent increases now and leave or renegotiate by about 2032. Related needs: (a) calculators and explainers for the new rent and legal deadlines; (b) help applying for state alternative units; (c) rental search outside the broker system; (d) moving, storage and renovation of units returned to owners; (e) owner tools to re-let recovered units. Owners getting units back (many old-rent units in central Cairo and Alexandria) will become new landlords who need listing, tenant screening, contracts and rent collection.
- **Monetization favors the landlord side.** Old-rent tenants are poorer, so landlord-side and owner-side products (verified listing plus digital contract plus rent collection and receipts) are likelier to pay than tenant-side apps. A tenant-side "verified rental search" could charge less than a broker's commission (norm unverified; see Gaps).
- **Old rent is a Greater Cairo and Alexandria story.** Products should launch there; the issue is minor in rural governorates (93% urban).

### Gaps
- Broker (سمسار) fee norms and practices, fake and outdated listing prevalence, deposit (تأمين) disputes, and contract registration practices for 2024–2026: **not researched** (search budget exhausted).
- Listing platforms' traffic and inventory (Aqarmap, Property Finder Egypt, Dubizzle/OLX Egypt, Bayut if present, Facebook groups) and their rental price reports for 2025–2026: **not researched**.
- Size of the "new rent" (إيجار جديد, Civil Code) market in households and its share in Greater Cairo vs other governorates: not found.
- Current (Oct 2026) implementation status of the old-rent law: area-classification committees, rent increases actually applied, court challenges, and uptake of the alternative-housing applications. **Not verified this session.**
- **Unverified background leads (NOT verified this session):**
  - The law was issued as **Law No. 164 of 2025** (ratified around early Aug 2025). It followed a **Supreme Constitutional Court ruling (Nov 2024)** that struck down fixed old rents.
  - Interim rent increases are reportedly **20x the current rent (minimum EGP 1,000) in "distinguished" areas** and **10x (minimum EGP 400 in medium areas, EGP 250 in economic areas)**, pending governorate classification committees, with **15% annual increases**.
  - An online platform to apply for **alternative state housing** opened around **Oct 2025** (Digital Egypt and post offices).
  - Common market norms: a broker's commission of about **one month's rent** for rentals and about **2.5%** for sales.
  - **OLX Egypt rebranded as Dubizzle** (2023).
  - CAPMAS's 2017 census counted roughly **12–13M vacant or closed housing units**, a large idle supply.

## 7. Pain-point opportunity matrix: scale, frequency, cost, workaround, existing solutions, software opportunity and monetization

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered, the best-evidenced mass-market opportunities are:
1. a **household bills and prepaid-meter copilot** (22.9M prepaid meters, monthly gas readings, about 13M broadband subscriptions, repeated tariff confusion and estimated-bill disputes);
2. a **government-procedure navigator plus document vault, with optional paid handling** for complex procedures (about 1.2M IDs and 1.3M birth certificates a month versus only about 10M Digital Egypt users).

**Old-rent transition services** are a well-evidenced but more segmented opportunity (1.8M households, concentrated in Greater Cairo and Alexandria). Building management and home services may be strong but are **unvalidated** in these notes.

Further synthesis:
- **The two "SMS-spend-tracker-like" ideas best supported by evidence:**
  - A **home bills copilot** built on meter-photo OCR plus reminders. It is low-ops, mass-market and monthly; it could start with prepaid electricity and gas readings.
  - A **government-papers navigator plus document vault** with optional paid handling for complex procedures. Its software-first design avoids the ops trap that likely limited earlier concierges.
- **The "AI passport photo"-style ideas** are productized one-off services with clear prices. On the evidence, they fit best within government procedures, e.g. "we prepare your complete file (photos, certified copies, translations, pre-filled forms) and book or deliver."
- **Geography:**
  - Old-rent and broadband products should launch in Greater Cairo and Alexandria.
  - Coded meters and reconciliation, and the civil-registry access pain (caravans), are relatively more pressing outside planned urban districts and in the governorates (inference).

### Gaps

- No direct user complaint mining was possible (app reviews, Reddit, Facebook groups) because fetches were blocked. This should be the first validation step.
- **Housing purchase, building management and home services need a dedicated follow-up research pass** once the search budget is raised. They are the weakest-evidenced parts of these notes.
