# Sell Egyptians trust, not another tracker

The strongest software opportunity in Egypt in October 2026 is not another spending tracker, because at least three apps already parse Egyptian bank alerts, led by Masareef, which reads messages from 130 banks, wallets and fintechs on the phone ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/masareef-expense-tracker/6760121459)). It is the same engine pointed at a problem people will pay to solve: proving to a seller that an InstaPay or wallet payment really arrived, and screening the scams built on fake proof. Online fraud makes up **59.7% of recorded cybercrime** ([Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5650864)), online selling drew about **35,000 Consumer Protection Agency complaints** in 2025, more than any product category ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/4/9/%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%D9%84%D9%83-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%B2-%D8%AA%D9%84%D9%82%D9%89-35-%D8%A3%D9%84%D9%81-%D8%B4%D9%83%D9%88%D9%89-%D8%AA%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%82-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%84%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9/7371779)), and InstaPay carried about **1.5 billion transfers worth EGP 2.9 trillion in 2024** ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/?p=102033)), yet no widely used Egyptian tool lets an informal seller confirm receipt instantly. Ranked behind it are a free "digital treasurer" for ordinary apartment buildings, where about three in four households live; fee reconciliation plus teacher-branded AI Baccalaureate practice for star tutors; a gehaz-and-qayma wedding planner paid by lenders and merchants; and a parent-care coordinator paid in dollars by Egyptians abroad, which has the clearest payer but is an operations business. Of nineteen candidates stress-tested against competitors, regulation and pricing, none scores above 3.5 out of 5, because Egypt's economics punish consumer subscriptions: proven mass-market prices stop at about **EGP 50–80 a month**, while profit pools sit in transaction fees, credit, merchant-side charges and hard-currency payers. Three forces shape every idea: the state keeps shipping free digital substitutes for information products, funded consolidators are moving into adjacent layers, and the data-protection law's grace period ends on **31 October 2026**. The right first step is cheap validation, shadowing sellers, treasurers and teachers and filing Google Play's SMS-permission declaration, before much code is written.

## Egypt's deepest frictions hide in coping systems, not complaint data

Money strain is the one problem nearly every Egyptian shares. In Arab Barometer's September 2025 fieldwork, about **60% of Egyptians could not afford enough food in the previous month**, including 38% of university graduates ([Arab Barometer](https://www.arabbarometer.org/2026/05/egyptian-public-opinion-factsheet/)). The 2026 war shock then delivered a second squeeze in about 30 months: fuel rose EGP 3 a litre on 10 March ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2026/03/10/egypt-raises-fuel-prices-by-egp-3-per-litre/)), household electricity rose about 12% on average in August ([EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/08/02/household-electricity-tariffs-hiked-by-an-average-of-12/)), and inflation stood at **12.7% nationally and 14.5% in cities** that month ([Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/576399.aspx)). The minimum wage rose to EGP 8,000 a month in July, about $158 ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2026/04/02/egypt-raises-minimum-wage-to-8000-egp-from-july-2026/)), while the average private-sector wage of EGP 5,796 (2024) sits below the roughly EGP 11,000 a family of four needs to stay above the poverty line ([Maspero](https://www.maspero.eg/economy/2026/01/06/918678/الإحصاء26.6-زيادة-في-متوسط-الأجر-الشهري-للعاملين-بالقطاع-العام-2024); [Al Manassa](https://almanassa.com/en/stories/25941)). The pain is universal; the household budget for software is not.

Official complaint channels show where the state fails rather than where software can help. The Cabinet's unified system handled about **2 million complaints and requests in 2025**, with housing and utilities the top sector at roughly 372,000 ([Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5527039.aspx)), and monthly volumes in mid-2026 ran well above the 2025 average, at 229,000 in July ([Aswaq Information](https://www.aswaqinformation.com/253324)). The Consumer Protection Agency logged **211,732 complaints in 2025**, led by online selling, durable goods and appliances (32,348) and phones ([Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5364204)), and the telecom regulator handled 263,262 escalations, with fixed-internet complaints up 32% between the first and second halves of the year ([Al Anbaa Al Aqaria](https://anbaaq.com/106318/2025/09/01/); [Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5473593)). The most actionable signal is behavioural: **88% of telecom complaints arrive by phone call** and only about 7% through the regulator's app or WhatsApp ([El Mydan News](https://www.elmydannews.com/244726)), so products for this market must be voice- and WhatsApp-first rather than app-first.

The richer signal lies in the parallel systems Egyptians built to cope, which they rarely describe as problems at all. Private tutoring is a shadow school system reaching **81% of secondary, 64% of preparatory and 56% of primary students** ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2026/04/09/why-private-tutoring-rules-egypts-education/)). Rotating savings circles (gam'eya) drew 43% of Egyptians in a 2018 study ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2025/11/23/el-gameya-remains-a-financial-lifeline-for-many-egyptians/)), and MoneyFellows digitized the habit for 8.5 million users ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/04/moneyfellows-raises-13m-to-take-its-group-savings-model-outside-egypt)). Some 90–95% of property is unregistered and held mostly on ʿurfi contracts ([Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2023/06/29/95-of-egypts-real-estate-is-unregistered-eba/)), shopkeepers hand out sweets instead of scarce coins ([Parlmany](https://www.parlmany.com/News/7/103541/%D9%84%D8%BA%D8%B2-%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%81%D9%83%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B5%D8%B1)), half of private establishments are informal ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2025/11/26/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%B3-%D8%AE%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%B7%D8%A9-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D9%8A%D8%AA%D8%BA%D9%8A%D8%B1-3-9-%D9%85%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%86/7212299)), and women spend **5.4 hours a day on unpaid care** ([ERF](https://erf.org.eg/publications/unpaid-care-work-in-egypt-gender-gaps-in-time-use/)), with working mothers describing how stress-laden school WhatsApp groups pull them into studying alongside their children ([Akhbar El-Yom](https://akhbarelyom.com/news/newdetails/4707654/1/-الماميز-الصداع-المطلوب)). Wherever an informal system already moves volume and money (tutoring fees, building dues, wedding budgets, peer-to-peer payments), software can replace the paper and add the ingredient that is almost always missing: trust.

Most of these changes push risk or paperwork from the state onto households: price risk under cash subsidies, payment proof under the rent law, eligibility tracking under the Baccalaureate. Each new program also hands scammers a fresh script. That is why trust and record-keeping products recur throughout the ranking that follows.

## Sellers, lenders and the diaspora pay where households cannot

Egyptian consumers do pay for digital services, but under a hard ceiling. Spotify charges **EGP 79 a month** ([Spotify](https://www.spotify.com/eg-en/premium)), Shahid VIP about EGP 50 ([pricesworld](https://www.pricesworld.net/?p=67720)), Netflix's help page lists EGP 70–165 ([Netflix](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/24926/EG)), and Talabat Pro costs EGP 79; Talabat called Egypt its most successful Pro launch ([Talabat](https://corporate.talabat.com/news/corporate-website-news-item-6); [Talabat IR](https://ir.talabat.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/20250514_talabat-Investor-Presentation.pdf)). At about EGP 51 to the dollar, that is $1–1.60 a month. Only 7% of Egypt's top-grossing Google Play apps come from Egyptian developers ([42matters](https://42matters.com/top-grossing-mobile-apps-egypt)), and 48% of Egyptian developers rely on in-app ads against 10% on in-app purchases ([42matters](https://42matters.com/how-do-egyptian-mobile-app-developers-monetize-their-apps)). Above roughly EGP 100 a month, a consumer app is a premium product.

The large profit pools sit in transactions, credit, the merchant side and remittances. Fawry earned EGP 8.65 billion in 2025 on EGP 943.6 billion of throughput, about **0.9% of what flows through it** ([Invest-Gate](https://invest-gate.me/news/fawry-posts-record-fy2025-results-with-57-revenue-growth/)); valU's revenue equalled about 23% of its EGP 24.5 billion GMV, mostly financing income ([Decypha](https://decypha.com/en/news/details/Valu-closes-2025-with-81--higher-net-income--revenues-hit-EGP-5-6bn/21539339?L=EN)); and InstaPay turned profitable a year after adding a 0.1% fee, without a significant fall in users ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/north-africa/egypt-instapay-turns-profitable-a-year-after-transfer-fee-rollout-rt18pyrt)). MoneyFellows charges early-payout members a one-time fee of about 6% and is profitable, though only about 350,000 of its 8 million-plus users are monthly active ([sme10x](https://sme10x.com/entrepreneurship-2/10x-focus-moneyfellows-fintech-startup-digitising-a-traditional-practice); [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/2025/10/21/egypts-money-fellows-surpasses-1-5-billion-in-transactions-achieves-profitability/)). Vezeeta charges doctors rather than patients ([Vezeeta](https://www.vezeeta.com/ar)), and Khazna sells earned-wage access through 400-plus employers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/04/egypts-khazna-banks-16m-for-its-financial-super-app-and-expansion-into-saudi/)). The cautionary tales point the same way. Telda's active users stayed near 500,000 between 2023 and early 2026, and it has since pivoted toward credit ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/25/egypts-telda-abandons-the-unbanked-to-chase-egypts-consumer-credit-boom/)); India's SMS tracker Walnut ended up as a lender's acquisition for about $30 million ([Retail Banker International](https://retailbankerinternational.com/news/walnut-acquisition-values-30m)); Khatabook spent ₹230 crore to earn ₹103 crore in FY24 ([YourStory](https://cwv.yourstory.com/2024/11/ms-dhoni-backed-khatabook-clocks-rs-1027-cr-revenue-cuts-losses-7-in-fy24)); and free ledger clones Kippa and Lummo wound down ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/02/23/kippa-users-left-in-the-dark/); [DealStreetAsia](https://www.dealstreetasia.com/stories/lummo-may-close-operations-340722)). By contrast, MyGate broke even by selling advertising to 3.5 million gated-community homes ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=550455)), and Classplus earned ₹213 crore charging tutors ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/2024/10/classplus-revenue-spikes-2x-to-rs-260-cr-in-fy24-cuts-losses-by-57)). The rule for Egypt is to charge whoever already moves money (sellers, teachers, vendors, lenders) or whoever earns in hard currency; remittances reached a record **$47.3 billion in FY2025/26** ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/21/egyptian-expat-remittances-hit-record-473-bln-usd-in-fy-2025-2026)).

Reaching people is cheap; earning their trust is not. Facebook CPMs in Egypt run about **$1–2.80**, among the lowest in the region ([Stackmatix](https://stackmatix.com/blog/facebook-ads-cpm-by-country-2026)), against roughly 50 million adults reachable on each of Facebook, YouTube and TikTok ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-egypt)). The winners grew through word of mouth and small, activation-gated referrals: Breadfast caps rewards at EGP 50 and pays a referrer only after five orders ([Breadfast](https://freshdesk.breadfast.com/en/support/solutions/articles/69000775519)). Accepting money no longer requires the startup's own licence. Kashier exposes InstaPay QR codes and request-to-pay with webhook confirmation, though without recurring billing ([Kashier](https://developers.kashier.io/docs/accept-payments/instapay)); Paymob charges 2.75% plus EGP 3 per card payment, which is 6% of an EGP 50 charge ([Paymob](https://paymob.com/en/pricing)); and Google Play billing takes 15% on subscriptions ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10632485)) and reaches unbanked users through carrier billing on Vodafone and e& ([Arabian Reseller](https://arabianreseller.com/2020/05/04/tpay-mobile-and-vodafone-egypt-launch-digital-payment-on-google-play/); [The Fintech Times](https://thefintechtimes.com/tpay-bango-and-etisalat-egypt-launch-direct-carrier-billing-for-the-google-play-store/)), while Egypt is ineligible for Play's alternative billing ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13821247)).

Building is cheap too, but capital is scarce. The median Egyptian software engineer earns about EGP 41,500 a month ([levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/t/software-engineer/locations/egypt)), employer social insurance is capped near EGP 3,131 a month ([RemotePass](https://www.remotepass.com/country/egypt/payroll)), and a WhatsApp utility message costs about $0.0036 ([ChatMaxima](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/egypt/)). Meanwhile, deal counts fell from 160 in 2022 to 69 VC deals in 2025, and fintech plus e-commerce took about 83% of H1 2026 dollars ([The Startup Scene](https://thestartupscene.me/INVESTMENTS/Egypt-s-Startups-Record-517-Million-in-Funding-in-2022); [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/12/19/how-egypts-startup-ecosystem-fared-in-2025/); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2651443/business-economy)). A small team should plan to live on revenue, incorporate locally through GAFI in about seven business days ([Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/guides/africa-corporate/egypt/incorporation-process/how-long-does-the-company-incorporation-process-take.html)), and use the February 2026 Startup Charter's 0.4–1.5% turnover tax for firms under EGP 20 million ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/02/egypt-launches-first-national-startup-charter)).

Three walls stop most ideas before they start. First, **the state is a free competitor** for information products: Digital Egypt reached 10.7 million users in 2025 ([Al Mal](https://almalnews.com/2092144/)) and delivers a renewed national ID to the door for EGP 50–175 ([Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5261716)), the drug authority began activating national track-and-trace in February 2026, starting with imported drugs ([Al Bawaba News](https://www.albawabhnews.com/5315857)), the cabinet runs a crowdsourced food Price Radar ([Business Today Egypt](https://businesstodayegypt.com/amp/1/4400/)), and the Ministry of Education publishes its own textbook-grounded AI tutor, Faheem ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6743378136)). Second, **funded consolidators** sit beside many gaps, from Abwaab buying teacher-software maker Eduact ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2026/09)) to Dubizzle absorbing Hatla2ee ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/hatla2eecom)). Third, **compliance arrives this month**. When the Personal Data Protection Law's grace period ends on 31 October 2026, data controllers need a licence, a registered data protection officer and 72-hour breach notification ([Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/); [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/01/egypt-regulatory-update-on-data-privacy)); hosting on a foreign cloud needs a separate cross-border licence, though fees are waived below 100,000 records ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/compliance/1845112/from-compliance-to-capital-how-egypts-pdpl-shapes-data-valuation)), and Huawei Cloud Cairo is the only in-country hyperscaler region ([IntelligentCIO](https://www.intelligentcio.com/africa/2024/05/23/huawei-cloud-announces-cairo-region/)). The central bank's June 2025 framework also licenses any "access to payment account information", and full e-money institutions need EGP 30 million in capital ([Matouk Bassiouny](https://matoukbassiouny.com/egypts-central-bank-issues-the-licensing-framework-for-psos-and-psps/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/fintech/1755348/electronic-money-institution-licensing-in-egypt)), so a small team must never hold customer funds or pull bank data through APIs.

Seen through this lens, the founder's two reference examples teach different lessons. The spending tracker proves the technology, since Android carries 88–91% of Egypt's mobile web traffic and on-device SMS parsing therefore reaches most users ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/egypt)). It also shows how fast obvious ideas get copied: Masareef, TrackNest and PennyWise already parse Egyptian bank alerts ([Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/boogado66/project/masareef); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6759209389); [F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/pt/packages/com.pennywiseai.tracker)), CIB's app categorizes spending ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6618151705)), and Klivvr added an AI spending assistant for about 700,000 users ([MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=40083)). The tracker's classic exit into lending is closing as well, because Google Play bars loan apps from reading SMS ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821)). The passport-photo service shows the other half of the pattern, an AI-made artifact with a paid physical or legal output, but the research could not establish whether Egyptian civil-status and passport offices accept user-supplied photos, so that model is not ranked here. The formula that survives keeps the first example's engine and the second example's paid artifact, and points both at the side of a transaction that has money.

## Five ideas survive the evidence, led by payment proof for sellers

The ranking weighs how many people hit the problem and how often, whether someone with money will pay, how open the field is, whether two to four people can ship an MVP in about four months without a licence, and whether a dated change makes the problem urgent now. Scores are judgments on a five-point scale. Every willingness-to-pay figure below is a hypothesis to test, competitor download counts could not be collected, and much of the evidence comes from news summaries rather than primary data.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Digital treasurer for ordinary apartment buildings | ~3 in 4 households live in apartments; dues every month | Maintenance vendors; optional paid building tier | Low to medium | 3 | Field-check 20–30 Cairo buildings |

### 1. Payment proof for sellers turns the SMS engine into a paid product

**Who has the problem, how often, and why it persists.** Egypt moved everyday money onto instant rails faster than it built trust into them. The telecom regulator counted **46.3 million active mobile wallets carrying 717.7 million transactions in Q2 2025** ([MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/2025/09/16/egypts-mobile-wallet-transactions-surge-72-in-q2-2025-to-egp-943-4-billion/)), InstaPay passed 11.5 million users by late 2024 ([Business Today Egypt](https://www.businesstodayegypt.com/Tag/66047/InstaPay/1)), and InstaPay QR codes arrived in June 2024 with merchant payments to follow ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2026/01/04/inside-egypts-instapay-economy-how-instant-payments-are-changing-access-for-a-new-generation/)). On the receiving side sit roughly **2.1 million wholesale and retail establishments**, in an economy where half of all private establishments are informal ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2025/11/26/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%B3-%D8%AE%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%B7%D8%A9-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D9%8A%D8%AA%D8%BA%D9%8A%D8%B1-3-9-%D9%85%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%86/7212299)), plus an uncounted mass of Facebook and Instagram sellers. Every sale paid by transfer contains a moment when the seller, often with a customer waiting, must decide whether a screenshot is real; for an active seller that moment comes daily. Fraud has industrialized around it. Police arrested a gang that showed shop staff fake payment-app screenshots ([Akhbar El Yom](https://m.akhbarelyom.com/news/NewDetails/4856118/1/سقوط-تشكيل-عصابي-للنصب-على-أصحاب-المحال-)), a ring broken up in March 2026 took **EGP 50,000–80,000 from each victim** through fake "verification transfers" ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Buzz/Criminal-Ring-Arrested-Over-Fraud-Operation-Targeting-InstaPay-Users)), the central bank warned in September 2026 about callers impersonating InstaPay staff ([Amwal Al Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/egypts-central-bank-warns-of-instapay-scams-targeting-users/)), and EnterpriseAM describes the trade as a criminal industry "with KPIs and a mission statement" ([EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/07/06/egypts-instapay-scams-arent-random-theyre-the-product-of-a-criminal-industry-with-kpis-and-a-mission-statement/)). Buyers suffer too: the Consumer Protection Agency calls anonymous social-media sellers the biggest risk consumers face ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/4/9/%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%D9%84%D9%83-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%B2-%D8%AA%D9%84%D9%82%D9%89-35-%D8%A3%D9%84%D9%81-%D8%B4%D9%83%D9%88%D9%89-%D8%AA%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%82-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%84%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9/7371779)). Yet the Interior Ministry received only about 1,200 e-fraud reports in Q1 2024 ([Mohamah.net](https://www.mohamah.net/law/?p=129704)), the signature of a latent pain absorbed in silence rather than complained about. It persists because instant payments confirm receipt only inside the payee's own banking or wallet app, many informal sellers take money into personal accounts with no merchant dashboard, and no shared reputation layer exists for the phone numbers and wallet IDs used in trades.

**Who already tries to solve it.** Nobody owns the payee side. Truecaller, with 100 million monthly users across the Middle East and Africa, flags spam calls rather than payment proof ([MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/2025/08/11/truecaller-surpasses-100-million-monthly-active-users-in-mea-region/)). Paymob and Kashier payment links, including Kashier's InstaPay request-to-pay with webhook confirmation ([Kashier](https://developers.kashier.io/docs/accept-payments/instapay)), help sellers willing to onboard with a gateway and pay its fees, but they do nothing for transfers that land directly in personal accounts. Escrow Pay, a Cairo escrow startup founded in 2022, shows no visible traction ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/escrow-pay-vs-waffy-app)); Dubizzle takes no payments and tells users to pay face to face ([Dubizzle](https://support.dubizzle.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408117970065-Scam-alert-do-not-share-your-credit-card-information)); banks issue warnings. Regional precedent shows that trust layers sell when anchored to banks or marketplaces, since Saudi Arabia's Waffy has processed more than 500,000 escrow transactions with Al Rajhi and GIB ([Money20/20 Middle East](https://app.money2020middleeast.com/event/money2020/exhibitor/RXhoaWJpdG9yXzIyNzMyMjc=)), while Nigeria's Middletrust shows the trap of percentage fees on small tickets, charging 22.8% on a ₦1,000 deal ([Technext](https://technext.ng/2020/05/23/middletrust-is-protecting-nigerians-from-payment-fraud-using-escrow-technology-but-its-fees-favour-large-transactions/)). The enabling technology already exists in Masareef's on-device parser ([Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/boogado66/project/masareef)); no one has pointed it at sellers.

**Product, MVP, revenue and go-to-market.** The MVP is an Android seller app, buildable in 8–12 weeks, that reads the seller's own bank and wallet SMS and notifications on the phone and answers one question in a tap or a WhatsApp message: did EGP 1,250 from this sender arrive? It matches credits to open orders, flags any screenshot with no matching credit, keeps a receipt log the seller can share with buyers, and lets sellers look up and report scam numbers and wallet IDs with evidence attached. A free WhatsApp bot that checks a number, link or screenshot for anyone gives the general public a reason to engage and feeds the reputation graph. Revenue starts with a seller subscription, testing **EGP 49–99 a month** around the EGP 79 anchor that Talabat Pro and Spotify have proven, billed through Google Play so the fixed EGP 3 card fee does not eat small charges. Later layers are partner-run "pay after inspection" escrow at 1–3% through a licensed payment provider, an API for couriers and marketplaces, and fraud-signal partnerships with the banks and wallets that carry fraud losses, which is also the route to a family "guardian mode" that protects elderly parents. Go-to-market starts where tickets are high and transfers person-to-person: phone and electronics shops, Instagram fashion and homeware sellers, and Dubizzle power sellers in Cairo and Giza, recruited with short videos of caught fakes on Facebook and TikTok and a referral credit paid only after a referred seller's first verified sale.

**Constraints, risks and what to validate first.** Google Play lets an app read SMS only under approved exceptions. "SMS-based money management" is on the list, but approval requires a declaration with a video demo, can take weeks and is discretionary; one indie finance app was rejected because manual entry existed as an alternative ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820?hl=en); [Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9214102?hl=en); [dev.to](https://dev.to/zeta_byte/ive-been-working-on-a-personal-finance-app-called-finvantage-and-i-recently-hit-a-roadblock-4mjh)). New personal developer accounts must first run a 12-tester, 14-day closed test ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)), Android 17 will delay messages containing one-time codes by three hours, which should leave ordinary payment alerts untouched ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-all?hl=en)), and iOS, with 9–12% of users, cannot read SMS at all, so notification parsing and alert forwarding must work as fallbacks. The central bank's licensing of "access to payment account information" does not address on-device parsing of a user's own messages, so a written legal opinion should precede launch, and the product must never hold funds. Under the data-protection law, financial data likely counts as sensitive, and a crowd-sourced database of phone numbers is personal data with defamation exposure, so parsing stays on the phone and every public report needs evidence and an appeal path. The biggest strategic risk is that InstaPay or the banks add a shareable, verifiable receipt; the others are parsers breaking when banks change message formats, false positives, and sellers declining to pay for something they can check by hand. Validate in this order: shadow 20–30 sellers for a week to count fake-proof attempts and minutes spent checking; run a concierge version in which sellers forward alerts to a WhatsApp bot and try to pre-sell 50 of them; file the Play declaration with a prototype in week one; and commission the central-bank and data-protection legal memo.

### 2. A free digital treasurer for buildings run from notebooks

**Who has the problem, how often, and why it persists.** Most urban Egyptians share a building, and the building's money runs on trust and a notebook. About **75.7% of households lived in apartments** in the 2017 census sample ([ERF](https://erfdataportal.com/index.php/catalog/248/variable/V2360)), across 13.5 million residential buildings ([Invest-Gate](https://invest-gate.me/news/egypts-capmas-reveals-latest-population-housing-census/)), which would be roughly 20 million of today's 26.5 million households if that share holds ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/buzz/over-half-of-egyptian-households-live-in-rural-areas)). Law 119/2008 requires an occupants' union in any building with five or more units ([Unitify](https://unitify.com/blog/unions-of-occupants-egypt-new-cities-maintenance-fees)), yet an Akhbar El-Yom column titled "the tragedy of occupants' unions" suggests most exist only on paper ([Akhbar El-Yom](https://m.akhbarelyom.com/news/newdetails/4808370/1/)), and the legal penalty for not paying dues is only EGP 10–100 a month ([El Balad](https://www.elbalad.news/5796336)). So every month a volunteer treasurer collects cash for the doorman, cleaning, water pumps and the elevator, records it in a notebook or a WhatsApp group, and absorbs the free-riding and suspicion. The stakes are not trivial: a child died in an El-Salam elevator in October 2025, and MPs are demanding enforcement of maintenance contracts ([Misr Connect](https://misrconnect.com/en/news/mobile-phones-and-accessories-stores/child-dies-in-el-salam-elevator); [Akhbar El Yom](https://akhbarelyom.com/news/newdetails/4792651/1/)). Few residents describe this as a problem a product could fix, which is exactly what makes it latent. The timing has turned: on 3 August 2026 the housing minister reviewed draft amendments that reorganize occupants' unions and tighten oversight ([Masrawy](https://www.masrawy.com/news/realestate-news/details/2026/8/3/3027283/)), and Saudi Arabia's Mullak platform shows what formalization can do, with **17,000 approved associations**, 3,600 of them created in the first half of 2025 alone ([REGA](https://rega.gov.sa/en/media-center/news/rega-owners-associations-record-185-growth-in-the-first-half-of-2025/)).

**Who already tries to solve it.** Funded players sell to compounds and developers, not ordinary buildings. Lyve (formerly iCommunity) raised a $600,000 Series A and serves 50-plus compounds with about 40,000 units ([Startup Bahrain](https://startupbahrain.com/blog/icommunity-the-community-management-platform-raises-600k-in-series-a-funding-from-algebra-ventures)), Unitify sells billing and gate access to compound managers ([Unitify](https://unitify.com/egypt)), and big developers run their own resident apps. For the typical 10–40-unit Cairo building there is only 3amaraPlus, a free iPhone-only app with no visible usage ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/3amaraplus/id6599847846)), union-accounting Excel templates sold on Khamsat ([Khamsat](https://khamsat.com/business/erp/1766271)), and a Mostaql job post asking a freelancer to build exactly this, down to a monthly doorman rating ([Mostaql](https://mostaql.com/project/140254)). Demand exists; supply does not.

**Product, MVP, revenue and go-to-market.** The MVP, three to four months of work on an Arabic Android app plus a WhatsApp bot, gives the treasurer a ledger with receipt photos, each apartment's dues and a who-paid board, automatic WhatsApp reminders, InstaPay and wallet payment links, maintenance tickets, notices and general-assembly votes, an elevator contract and inspection tracker, and a monthly PDF statement. Matching incoming transfers to apartments reuses the payment-proof engine from idea one. Card fees are punishing at this ticket size, since Paymob's 2.75% plus EGP 3 is about 3.7% of an EGP 300 fee ([Paymob](https://paymob.com/en/pricing)), so InstaPay at 0.1% should be the default. Residents use the product free. Revenue comes from the supply side, as it did for MyGate, which reached breakeven on ₹173.5 crore of FY25 revenue by advertising to 3.5 million homes ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=550455)): elevator maintenance contracts with compliance reminders, tank cleaning, pest control and repairs sold through a vetted vendor marketplace, plus an optional paid tier per building (Indian peers charge ₹15–25 per flat a month, according to [Codingclave](https://codingclave.com/guides/society-management-app-development-cost-india-2026)). Go-to-market runs treasurer by treasurer through doormen, landlords and elevator companies, starting with elevator buildings in Cairo, Giza and Alexandria, and adds registration and compliance flows the day the amendment text is published.

**Constraints, risks and what to validate first.** The product must never pool residents' money; collecting through InstaPay or a licensed gateway keeps it outside central-bank licensing. Union data falls under the data-protection law, though most deployments will sit far below the 100,000-record fee threshold. The risks are real: no evidence yet that anyone pays, a cash culture, treasurers who prefer opacity, WhatsApp plus Excel being "good enough", churn when a treasurer steps down, and the possibility that the state launches its own Mullak-style platform, which would be a threat or, handled well, a channel. That funded players chose compounds is itself a warning. Validate first with a field check of 20–30 Cairo and Giza buildings to learn actual monthly fees, doorman wages, elevator-contract prices and how dues are collected today; run ten buildings on a WhatsApp-and-spreadsheet concierge for two months to see whether on-time payment improves; and ask three to five elevator firms whether they would pay for qualified leads.

### 3. Fee reconciliation and branded AI practice for star tutors

**Who has the problem, how often, and why it persists.** Tutoring is Egypt's largest cash-based coordination problem. With **28.9 million pre-university students** ([Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5303782)) and tutoring rates of 56–81% by stage, an estimated 16–23 million students take private lessons, at **EGP 150–200 a session** in 2025 and about EGP 30,000 for a Thanaweya Amma year; the ministry put the market at EGP 47 billion in FY2021/22, before three years of inflation ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2026/04/09/why-private-tutoring-rules-egypts-education/)). Sessions are weekly, fees monthly, homework daily. About 1.03 million teachers work in pre-university schools ([Ahram Gate](https://gate.ahram.org.eg/News/5298710.aspx)), and the stars among them run hundreds of students across several centres with assistants, paper registers and, reportedly, payment screenshots checked by hand, while mothers juggle several teachers through overloaded WhatsApp groups ([Akhbar El-Yom](https://akhbarelyom.com/news/newdetails/4707654/1/-الماميز-الصداع-المطلوب)). The problem persists because the system is cash-based and semi-legal, so no formal software vendor has standardized it. The Egyptian Baccalaureate adds urgency: 92% of Grade 10 students chose it ([Egypt Telegraph](https://www.egypttelegraph.com/article/206570/%D9%85%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%B4-%D8%A5%D8%AC%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%8C-%D9%88%D8%B2%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85-92-%D9%85%D9%86)); from 2026/27 students must complete 60% of weekly and monthly assessments to sit the free first attempt, July resits cost EGP 200 per subject, and the highest mark counts ([Elwatan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/8133886)). About 921,709 students sat the 2026 Thanaweya Amma ([Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5649658)), and the first university admissions under the new system will have no historical cut-off scores, which means year-round practice demand from anxious families.

**Who already tries to solve it.** The software layer is crowded but shallow. Local tools such as Hader, Amedra and Edu QR (sold as a lifetime licence with no monthly fee) handle attendance and fee tracking ([Hader](https://www.hadereg.com/); [Amedra](https://amedra.net/); [Edu QR](https://www.eduqr.dev/en/)), and freelancers build custom centre systems with barcode attendance and "who paid" screens ([Khamsat](https://khamsat.com/programming/desktop-app/4142973-%D8%AA%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%82-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%83%D8%B2-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%A9)). In September 2026 Jordan's Abwaab bought Eduact, an Egyptian platform for independent teachers and centres ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2026/09)). On the AI side, the Ministry of Education publishes Faheem, a tutor grounded in government textbooks ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6743378136)), the state is building sovereign Arabic AI apps ([Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/576301.aspx)), and free general chatbots already solve photographed problems. What nobody verifiably does well is the money-and-trust loop: automatically matching each InstaPay or wallet payment to the right student and group, sending official-API WhatsApp reports to parents, splitting a teacher's revenue across centres, and generating Baccalaureate-format practice and grading under the teacher's own brand.

**Product, MVP, revenue and go-to-market.** Sell to the star teacher with 300-plus students, not the centre owner. The MVP combines groups and schedules, QR attendance scanned by phone, a balance ledger with automatic transfer matching (again the payment-proof engine), payment links, and WhatsApp parent reports at about $0.0036 per utility message ([ChatMaxima](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/egypt/)). A second release adds AI-generated practice sets and grading in Baccalaureate format, grounded in ministry textbooks with answer keys the teacher reviews. Charge per active student, testing **EGP 5–10 a month**, or EGP 1,500–3,000 for a 300-student teacher, a small slice of fees running at EGP 150–200 per session. India's Classplus shows the model at scale, with ₹213 crore of FY24 operating revenue from tutors ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/2024/10/classplus-revenue-spikes-2x-to-rs-260-cr-in-fy24-cuts-losses-by-57)). Go-to-market is field sales to named teachers in Cairo, Giza and Delta cities during the August–October sign-up season; each teacher brings hundreds of parents, and a parent view spanning all of a child's teachers creates the network effect a single-teacher tool lacks.

**Constraints, risks and what to validate first.** The customers operate in a grey zone. The ministry ordered tutoring centres closed indefinitely, though they keep operating ([Al Bawaba News](https://www.albawabhnews.com/5387608)); it dropped licensing plans in November 2022 ([The National](https://thenational-the-national-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/mena/egypt/2022/11/03/egypt-backtracks-on-licensing-private-tutorial-centres)); an MP proposed licensing and taxing centres in April 2026 ([Masrawy](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2026/4/19/2974786/)); and governorates still seal centres, such as 21 in Ismailia ([Dostor](https://www.dostor.org/5525811)). Fear of a digital money trail is the biggest adoption risk, so the product must log cash as easily as transfers. The data-protection regulations require guardian consent for children under 15 ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins)). Other risks are lifetime-licence price anchors, Abwaab's capital, free state AI, curriculum churn and a cheating backlash against photo-solving. Validate first by interviewing 20 teachers with 300-plus students about assistant costs, unpaid fees and attitudes to digital records; piloting automated reconciliation with three teachers for one term; and testing AI accuracy on 200 Baccalaureate-format questions against teacher-verified keys.

### 4. A gehaz-and-qayma planner funded by lenders and merchants

**Who has the problem, how often, and why it persists.** Every Egyptian marriage starts with a multi-year, two-family negotiation over money. Egypt registered **936,739 marriage contracts in 2024**, 57.8% of them rural ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Buzz/Egypt-Records-Decline-in-Marriages-Uptick-in-Divorces-in-2024)), so roughly 1.9 million parental households finance a wedding each year. Media benchmark the total at about EGP 1 million ([Masrawy](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_economy/details/2026/6/9/3000108/)), and even a simple shabka (engagement gold) now tops **EGP 100,000**, against EGP 15,000–20,000 in 2018 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.net/ebusiness/2025/5/17/%d9%85%d9%86-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%b0%d9%87%d8%a8-%d8%a5%d9%84%d9%89-%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%81%d8%b6%d8%a9-%d9%87%d9%84-%d8%aa%d8%ba%d9%8a%d8%b1-%d8%a3%d8%b2%d9%85%d8%a7%d8%aa)). Installment credit carries much of the gehaz (household furnishings): consumer finance grew 57% to EGP 96.3 billion in 2025, with electronics and appliances about 35% of value ([MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/egypts-consumer-finance-sector-skyrockets-57-to-1-8-billion-fra-reports/)), across 10.7 million customers ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/01/15/why-egypts-10m-instalment-customers-have-become-africas-most-fought-over-fintech-asset/)). Who pays for what is negotiated verbally, differs between the Delta and Upper Egypt, and ends up in a paper qayma (the signed list of the bride's furnishings) that becomes evidence in disputes when marriages end; 273,892 couples divorced in 2024. New rules sharpen the pain: an August 2026 bill backed by 60 MPs would make qayma notarization mandatory ([Parlmany](https://www.parlmany.com/News/2/508555/)), the family-law overhaul tops the parliamentary session opening this month ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/9/17/%D8%AD%D8%B3%D9%85-%D9%85%D8%B5%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D9%82%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AD%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%AE%D8%B5%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%87%D9%84-%D8%AA%D9%8F%D8%B7%D9%84%D9%82-%D8%AC%D8%B1%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%AF%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A/7546798)), and on 16 September 2026 the Financial Regulatory Authority banned consumer-finance companies from financing gold ([Banker](https://banker.news/154249)), so the shabka must now be saved for rather than financed.

**Who already tries to solve it.** Pieces exist; the workflow does not. Farahy, a wedding-vendor marketplace, raised about $35,000 and covers the event, not the gehaz ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/489740-32)). valU runs 0% furniture-expo plans across its merchants ([EFG Holding](https://efghldg.com/media/news/valuxlemarchexelectrotech)), Contact sells wedding packages ([Arab Finance](https://arabfinance.com/en/news/newdetails/6116)), banks offer bride-furnishing loans ([Banker](https://banker.news/87654)), and gold apps show prices but not a shabka budget. Nobody combines the room-by-room list, the two-family budget split, price comparison and a matching installment offer, and the qayma remains paper, without photos or receipts.

**Product, MVP, revenue and go-to-market.** A free Arabic web app gives both families a shared checklist by room and social tier with who-pays tags, a running budget, price ranges drawn from public retailer listings and a daily gold feed, and a one-tap qayma PDF with photos, receipts and serial numbers that both sides sign. That PDF echoes the passport-photo pattern: an AI-assisted document with real legal weight. Revenue comes from referrals rather than couples. Merchant commissions of 2–5% on EGP 100,000-plus of appliances and furniture would yield roughly **EGP 2,000–10,000 per couple**, plus lead fees from BNPL providers and banks and an optional lawyer-reviewed qayma pack. Distribution follows the engagement season on TikTok and Facebook and runs through BNPL apps, appliance chains and Damietta furniture workshops, timed to the notarization bill.

**Constraints, risks and what to validate first.** The product must stay a non-lender referral partner to remain outside FRA licensing, and financing data should be treated as sensitive under the data-protection law. Retention is zero by design, since each couple uses it once, so acquisition cost must be recovered within a single purchase cycle, while BNPL providers hold the bargaining power and could copy the checklist. Prices go stale after currency moves, the qayma is socially sensitive, and mandatory notarization could favour an official e-service over a private one. Validate by securing referral terms from two or three BNPL providers and ten merchants before building, then running 30 engaged couples through a concierge version for one engagement season.

### 5. Dollar-paid parent care: the best payer, the heaviest operations

**Who has the problem, how often, and why it persists.** Egypt's money increasingly comes from abroad while its parents age at home. Remittances reached a record **$47.3 billion in FY2025/26** ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/21/egyptian-expat-remittances-hit-record-473-bln-usd-in-fy-2025-2026)) from roughly 9.5 million Egyptians abroad, 2.9 million of them in Saudi Arabia ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2025/07/21/egyptians-in-saudi-arabia-stories-of-opportunity-and-constraint/)), while **9.8 million Egyptians are aged 60 or over** ([Al Bawaba News](https://www.albawabhnews.com/5271392)). Care is hard to buy remotely: about 120,000 of 220,000 registered doctors work abroad ([Zawia3](https://zawia3.com/en/doctors-5/)), the nursing syndicate cites a shortfall of 75,000 nurses ([EnterpriseAM](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/blackboards/nursing-in-egypt-where-does-it-fall-short-and-how-can-it-be-improved/)), out-of-pocket payments exceed 60% of health spending ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8741917/)), drug prices are now reviewed every six months ([Elwatan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/8269662)), and scammers target the elderly before pension dates ([Akhbar El Yom](https://m.akhbarelyom.com/news/NewDetails/4873266/1/تحذير-هام-لمستخدمي-إنستاباي--حيلة-جديدة-)). Siblings coordinate weekly over family WhatsApp groups with no one accountable, which is why the need persists despite the money available to meet it.

**Who already tries to solve it.** Clinical pieces exist; coordination does not. 7keema offers on-demand home nursing on a roughly $250,000 seed ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/7keema)), Anees Health raised a seed round on 21 September 2026 for clinician-led home care in Greater Cairo ([Innovation Village](https://innovation-village.com/anees-health-raises-seed-funding-to-scale-integrated-in-home-care-model/)), Doxx raised $1.5 million in 2022 ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2022/05/healthtech-startup-doxx-launches-raising-1-5-million-seed-round)), and Yodawy handles chronic refills ([The Condia](https://thecondia.com/yodawy-secures-16-million-series-b/)). None sells one trusted coordinator to a child abroad. In Pakistan, parent-care plans for families abroad sell at $49 to $1,399 a month ([The Friday Times](https://www.thefridaytimes.com/20-Aug-2025/mohsyn-launches-as-a-premium-family-support-system-for-overseas-pakistanis)).

**Product, MVP, revenue and go-to-market.** Start manually on WhatsApp in one city (Greater Cairo or Alexandria) and one corridor (Saudi Arabia or Kuwait). A dollar subscription, testing **$39–79 a month**, buys a weekly coordinator call or visit, medicine management with partner-pharmacy refills, bill payments, home nurses and labs at cost plus a margin (three home visits a week cost EGP 1,400–1,600, according to [Misr Connect](https://misrconnect.com/news/egypt-news/iv-drip-costs-and-prices-at-home-in-egypt-2025-2026)), an emergency line and a monthly report to the family. Customers come through Facebook groups of Egyptians in the Gulf, expat employers and remittance apps; the app comes later.

**Constraints, risks and what to validate first.** This is an operations business with software inside, not mass-market software. The Medical Liability and Patient Safety Law, in force since 29 October 2025, creates complaint committees and mandatory insurance ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/press-releases/egypts-new-medical-liability-law-2025/)); health data is sensitive under the data-protection law, and sharing it with a child abroad is a cross-border transfer; and the company should partner with licensed providers rather than employ clinicians. One bad nurse can destroy trust, supply thins outside Cairo and Alexandria, and churn follows every recovery or death. Validate by pre-selling 30 Gulf-based families and running them manually for three months to measure retention and gross margin per family. Pursue it only if the founder wants a service company; it has the clearest payer of the five.

Three patterns explain the failures. Where the state can publish the information for free (stock levels, fines, prices, curriculum), private information products lose. Where the job requires people in the field at low ticket sizes (technicians, inspectors, runners, mappers), the platform keeps too little per job to pay for acquisition. And where the money involves lending, insurance, gold or job placement, a licence wall stands in the way. Two of these failures deserve a steelman because they look like the broadest "why now" plays. A WhatsApp "digital service office" for the burst of new government processes touches tens of millions of people, but the state is closing the gap from the other side, issuing death and inheritance certificates online ([Maspero](https://www.maspero.eg/egypt/2025/02/18/843906/)) and digitizing consular services in August 2026 ([Egyptian Streets](https://egyptianstreets.com/2026/08/05/good-news-for-egyptians-abroad-consular-services-go-digital/)), which leaves an operations-heavy residue gated by powers of attorney and lawyers. A home-energy companion addresses near-universal bill shock, but recharge, payment and meter reading all belong to state-linked operators and Fawry, leaving an "explain and predict" layer few will pay for. A few candidates were not stress-tested and belong on a watch list: a cash-subsidy wallet companion for the 61–66 million beneficiaries, whose card design remains unknown and whose Port Said rollout drew reports of weak uptake ([El Nabaa](https://www.elnabaa.net/1155390/%D8%B3%D8%B1-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%85-%D8%A5%D9%82%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B7%D9%86%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%AF-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A8%D9%88%D8%B1%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%8A%D8%AF)); a co-parenting app that waits on the family law's final text; a shop credit ledger with e-receipt compliance, where free ledgers failed abroad and the Tax Authority already offers point-of-sale support to small taxpayers ([ETA](https://www.eta.gov.eg/ar/news/dmana-shamlana-wbla-hdwd-lshrakayna-almmwlynalmklfyn)); and the AI passport-photo model, whose core question, whether offices accept user-supplied photos, remains open.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing is that Egypt's informal economy reconciles payments by eye. Sellers do, and the evidence suggests tutors, building treasurers and gam'eya organizers do too: they receive person-to-person transfers into personal accounts and confirm them by checking apps and screenshots under time pressure, while fraudsters exploit the gap. A payee-side, on-device engine that matches incoming transfers to orders, students, apartments or members is therefore a horizontal building block, and it is the founder's own SMS-reading idea turned around: from spenders, who will not pay to watch money leave, to receivers, who will pay to be sure it arrived. That argues for a sequence rather than a single bet. Launch with sellers, where fraud makes the pain acute and the payer obvious, then reuse the engine for tutors and treasurers, whose collections are the same problem in different clothes, and test early whether those groups really reconcile by screenshot.

Timing favours whoever moves before the rules are written. The strongest tailwinds found here are drafts (the occupants'-union amendments, qayma notarization, the family law) that could turn soft pains into legal obligations, and a data-protection deadline that will reward startups that license early, because banks, telcos and lenders will need compliant partners once enforcement begins. Four developments would reorder this ranking: InstaPay or the banks shipping verifiable receipts, which would weaken idea one; Google Play rejecting the SMS declaration, which would push idea one toward notification parsing and alert forwarding; publication of the union amendment text, which could lift idea two to first place; and Abwaab bundling payments into Eduact, which would close much of idea three.
